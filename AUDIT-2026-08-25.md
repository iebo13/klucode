# KluCode Site Audit, 2026-08-25

Audited: `http://localhost:3000/de/` (dev server, plus a fresh production build), all eight German pages, EN homepage, light and dark theme, 1440x900 and 390x844. Compared against the three reference competitors from the August market research (neuzeitwerber.de, tobeworks.de, florian-strasser.de) and current UX research. This file records the state after the "Ink and Paper / liquid glass" redesign and is deliberately critical. The running design log stays in `AUDIT.md`.

## Verdict in one paragraph

The craft is now real: typography, copy, accessibility, SEO plumbing, privacy posture and bundle discipline are all above the market standard for this segment, and the copy voice is the best asset the site has. But the site still loses the comparison where it matters most, proof. All three competitors show faces, names, numbers or screenshots. KluCode shows a beach photo, an honesty panel apologizing for the missing testimonials, and zero pixels of the three systems it keeps talking about. Meanwhile the homepage spends roughly half its scroll height on a pinned 3D sequence whose models read as toy-like and which research says actively costs conversions. The engineering under the page is better than the argument on top of it.

Grades: copy A, design craft B+, tech/SEO A-, accessibility A-, performance B+, proof and trust D, conversion path C+, legal C (one launch blocker).

## What was verified and works

Worth stating, because most sites in this market get none of it right:

- Single H1 per page, clean H2/H3 outline, localized slugs, trailing-slash canonicals, full hreflang trio (de, en, x-default) in identical shape on every page, sitemap with 12 URLs, robots.txt, branded bilingual 404.
- JSON-LD: ProfessionalService, WebSite and FAQPage parse correctly.
- Full content is present in the static HTML export. The site is readable with JavaScript off.
- Contact form: proper labels, autocomplete attributes, honeypot field, required consent checkbox, inline errors with `aria-invalid` and `aria-describedby`, and a graceful failure state that shows the email address when the relay is unreachable. Posts to self-hosted `contact.php`, no third-party form service, matching the privacy claims in the footer and Datenschutzerklärung.
- FAQ uses native `details`/`summary`. Skip link exists in both languages. A single `:focus-visible` style is enforced globally.
- Contrast: a programmatic sweep of every text node on the homepage found zero elements below WCAG AA, with margin. The "WCAG-konforme Farbwerte" claim on the Ansatz page currently holds.
- The 3D scene refuses to mount on small viewports, under `prefers-reduced-motion`, and without WebGL, and each refusal collapses to a clean static layout. This is the right way to do it.
- Bundle discipline: 115 kB first-load JS, three.js code-split into a 143 kB deferred chunk under the project's own 155 kB cap, fonts self-hosted.
- Datenschutzerklärung is complete and specific. Dark and light themes both render correctly, including the glass nav.
- The EN side is not a translation but a separate positioning (capacity for agencies and product teams). That is the right call, not a bug.

## Critical findings

### C1. The proof gap is still the number one problem, and it is now load-bearing

The August research concluded the dissatisfaction was "the system plus missing proof". The system got rebuilt. The proof did not arrive:

- No testimonial anywhere. Not one named, located customer voice.
- No numbers. The case studies say "kostete jeden Monat mehrere Tage" before, but never one measured result after.
- No screenshots. Three shipped systems, zero pixels of any of them. The Projekte page even puts this in writing in the "Zu Zahlen und Namen" panel, which is honest and well-worded, but it is an apology standing where evidence should stand.
- No client logos, no review platform, no GitHub link, no external artifact of any kind. The one public deliverable, the Reinigungsunternehmen landing page, is described but not linked.

How others do this, concretely: neuzeitwerber.de leads with 5.0 stars from 125 Google reviews plus 289 ProvenExpert ratings, recognizable client logos, and twelve timestamped ranking wins with percentages. florian-strasser.de shows three named testimonials with photos and roles, ten portfolio case studies with hero images, and six client logos. Even tobeworks.de, the weakest of the three on proof, shows two detailed cases and a founder narrative. KluCode currently has the least proof of the four sites while making the most precise claims. For a visitor comparing tabs, precision without evidence reads as copywriting.

Priority action, in order of effort to impact: (1) screenshot or screen-record the three systems, anonymized if needed, and put them in the case studies and in the lens section, (2) get one written testimonial with name, trade and city from the three paying customers, (3) add one number per project, even a modest one. A single "Die Abrechnung dauert jetzt einen Vormittag statt vier Tage, Name, Betrieb, Stadt" outweighs every panel on the site.

### C2. The pinned crossroads section costs half the homepage and fights the visitor

Measured: the pinned scroll sequence occupies roughly 4,900 px of the homepage's 9,727 px at 1440x900. Half the page is one section that delivers one heading, four short paragraphs and a four-item price list. Between two screenshots 1,400 px of wheel input apart, the copy column advanced by about 20 px. The fourth offer (Betrieb & Wartung, the cheapest entry point and the retention product) is buried at the very end of the tunnel.

NN/g's usability research on scrolljacking found most participants at least mildly disoriented, some interpreting it as a bug, and task-focused visitors dropping off, which is a direct conversion cost ([Scrolljacking 101](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/scrolljacking-101/), [Customer Impact summary](https://www.customerimpact.be/en/blog/scroll-hijacking/)). The site's own target buyer, a Betrieb owner on a lunch break, is the definition of task-focused.

The section also has a cosmetic defect that undermines the craft story: the copy column is an `overflow-y: auto` container (`.crossroads-copy`) with default scrollbars, so on Windows and Linux a chunky native scrollbar with arrow buttons renders permanently inside the ink panel, next to the page scrollbar. Two visible scrollbars on a flagship section. Fix regardless of any other decision (`scrollbar-width: none` plus the WebKit equivalent, since the page drives the scrolling).

Recommendation: keep the concept, cut the runway by at least half so the whole sequence resolves in roughly two viewports, and make sure all four offers are reachable within the first viewport of the section. The degradations (mobile, reduced motion, no WebGL) are already excellent, which proves the static variant works. The static variant is arguably the better default.

### C3. The 3D objects are below the site's craft line, and they carry no information

The furniture-and-figures scenes (orange low-poly desks, simple blue figures, wireframe shelving) read as placeholder art next to Schibsted Grotesk and the hairline system. The site's own design doctrine, recorded in AUDIT.md, is that the house device must encode real information, which is exactly what the SystemDiagram on the Projekte teaser does well. The crossroads scenes encode almost nothing: a desk means "agency", monitors mean "software".

This connects directly to C1: the most valuable thing that could sit inside that lens is real product UI. Three.js is already paying its 143 kB to show clay furniture. Real screenshots of the CRM, the Kasse and the Landingpage, framed in the existing ink treatment, would simultaneously raise the craft level and close the proof gap. If real UI cannot be shown yet, raising the model quality (or reducing the scene to the wireframe-only language, which is the strongest of its current styles) matters, because right now the hero promise "Software, die tatsächlich arbeitet" is illustrated by software nobody can see.

### C4. The offer ladder still has the 2,500 to 9,000 gap

Unchanged since the August research: competitors' best-selling tiers cluster at 3,900 to 5,500 €, neuzeitwerber sells at exactly 2,500 € with installments, and KluCode jumps from 2,500 € directly to 9,000 €. The segment-A buyer who has outgrown a landing page but cannot sign a five-figure-adjacent number has no rung. A "Website mit System" tier around 4,500 to 5,500 € (site plus one workflow, one form-to-database path, one Auswertung) would sit exactly in the validated cluster. This is an owner decision, but the audit should record that the market data has not been acted on.

### C5. The founder photo works against the positioning

The Über mich photo is a vacation-style shot in a wolf-print t-shirt on a beach. The page around it argues engineer responsibility, B.Sc., "ich prüfe, teste und verantworte jede Zeile". florian-strasser and neuzeitwerber use work-context photography. A face is far better than no face, and the photo is likable, but for a 9,000 € trust decision by a German SMB owner the mismatch between voice (precise, sober) and image (beach holiday) is real. A plain portrait, same person, neutral background, would keep the warmth and drop the dissonance. Lower effort alternative until then: crop tight to the face.

### C6. Launch blocker: Impressum is incomplete

The imprint self-reports it: hosting provider name and address are missing from `src/content/profile.ts`, `check:profile` and `check:meta` fail on it, and the warning banner renders on the public page in both languages. Abmahnfähig in Germany, as the banner itself says. Nothing else on the site should ship before this is filled.

### C7. Contact friction is high for this segment by deliberate choice, and the choice deserves one more look

The site offers email and a form. No phone (removed by design), no WhatsApp, no bookable slot. The August research flagged WhatsApp as a validated trust device for segment A, and all three competitors publish a phone number, two of them WhatsApp. The promise "Ein Gespräch von 30 Minuten, telefonisch oder per Video" currently requires writing a message and waiting up to 24 hours for the possibility of scheduling. If phone stays out, a self-scheduling link for exactly that 30-minute call (self-hostable if third-party services are the objection) would remove the friction without reintroducing the interruption channel. As-is, the contact path filters for patient, text-first buyers, which is a real cost in Handwerk.

## Medium findings

- M1. The OG image is still the logo card set in the old Space Grotesk face. Known open task, now measurable: every share to WhatsApp or LinkedIn renders the previous identity. Regenerate logo SVG and OG in Schibsted Grotesk, and consider an OG card that carries the value proposition instead of only the wordmark.
- M2. Reproducibility: `three` was declared in package.json but absent from node_modules, so a clean production build failed until `npm install` (fixed during this audit). The dev server masked it via webpack cache. The committed `out/` was ten days stale. If deployment ever ships `out/` directly, that is a stale-deploy hazard. A CI step doing clean install, build, and the `check:*` suite would catch both.
- M3. Fonts total 353 kB across 11 woff2 files, one file alone 85 kB. Only three files are preloaded, so first paint is fine, but an audit of which weights and character sets are actually used would likely halve the payload. Relevant because the Leistungen checklist sells "Ladezeit unter einer Sekunde".
- M4. The availability badge "Freie Kapazität ab September" is hand-set and today is August 25. In a week it reads as "available now", in six weeks it reads stale. Stale availability is an anti-trust signal. Either automate the month or put a reminder in the release routine.
- M5. The Leistungen page has no FAQ and no testimonial slot, yet it is where price objections live. The six homepage FAQ answers (including "Was kostet das?") do not appear or link there. Cross-link at minimum.
- M6. The imprint warning list renders em dashes as bullets, and the site otherwise enforces the no-em-dash rule. Dev-only state, but the component contradicts the house style it guards.
- M7. Mobile drawer: no backdrop dim behind the panel, and the page behind stays visible and scrollable. Minor, but every competitor drawer closes off the page. Also the "Switch to English" and availability line share one cramped row with the theme toggle.
- M8. No `theme-color` meta, so mobile browser chrome does not match the ink surfaces.
- M9. EN homepage title says "Custom software, built in Düsseldorf" while the page sells developer capacity to agencies. Not wrong, but the query the EN page can actually win ("freelance react developer germany" class) is not in the title.

## User journey and heuristics pass

Walked as three journeys: the segment-A owner arriving from search or referral (mobile and desktop), the deliberate researcher using the nav in order, and the EN agency buyer. Then a sweep against the classic usability heuristics.

### J1. Price meets the visitor before proof does

Homepage order on desktop: hero, Ausgangslage, price board (inside the tunnel), and only then the delivered systems. A first-time visitor is asked to metabolize "ab 9.000 €" before seeing a single thing that was built. Both stronger competitors put credibility devices immediately after the hero and prices later. Price transparency is the right strategy for this segment, but transparency lands better on top of capability. Two cheap fixes, either works: swap the Projekte band ahead of the price board, or seed one proof line into each Weg in the crossroads copy ("Zuletzt geliefert: CRM und Abrechnung für eine Vertriebsorganisation"), which also starts the evidence chain J2 asks for. The hero chips ("Drei Systeme im Produktivbetrieb") are honest micro-proof but carry the whole burden right now.

### J2. Every proof path is a cul-de-sac

Verified in the content files: there is not a single internal link inside the German copy. No offer links to the project that proves it, no FAQ answer links to the page that answers it in full ("Was kostet das?" names prices but does not link Leistungen), and the case studies end blind, with no live URL, no screenshot, and no "passendes Angebot" pointer. The only forward paths on the site are the global nav and the section-footer arrow links. The journey the site wants (skeptic reads offer, checks the matching evidence, comes back convinced) is not wired. Fix is pure content: one "Beispiel:" link per Leistung to the matching case, one "Passendes Angebot:" link per case back, and links inside FAQ answers. An afternoon of work, no design changes.

### J3. The undecided visitor has no triage path

neuzeitwerber runs a 3-question quote flow, tobeworks a 5-question configurator. KluCode's answer to "which of the four Wege am I?" is the 30-minute call, which is consistent with the positioning, but the site never helps a visitor self-classify beyond one "Für ..." line per offer. A single sentence on Leistungen and Kontakt ("Unsicher, welcher Weg passt? Beschreiben Sie den Ablauf in zwei Sätzen, die Einordnung ist Teil des Gesprächs") would convert the doubt into the existing funnel instead of losing it. No configurator needed.

### J4. Heuristics sweep, results

- Visibility of system status: pass. The form runs idle, sending, sent, handoff and failed states with a disabled button and label swap (verified in `contact-form.tsx`).
- User control and freedom: the one failure, the crossroads tunnel (C2). Everything else on the site scrolls, focuses and toggles natively.
- Consistency and standards: pass, and notably good. The same two CTA labels and the same finale block appear on every page, so the scent never changes.
- Recognition over recall, error prevention, error recovery, help: all pass (five-item nav, form validation, graceful relay failure, FAQ).
- Expectation setting after conversion: pass, above market standard. "Was danach passiert" with three concrete steps on Kontakt is something none of the three competitors do.

The interaction layer is clean. The journey problems are not interaction defects, they are missing connections (J2), one ordering choice (J1), and missing proof to connect to (C1).

| Device | neuzeitwerber | florian-strasser | tobeworks | KluCode today |
|---|---|---|---|---|

| Client logos | yes, recognizable | 6 | mentioned clients | none |

| Founder face | team photo | yes | yes, professional | yes, beach photo |
| Phone / WhatsApp | both | phone | phone, WhatsApp | neither |
| Instant quote / booking | 3-question quote in 24h | free consultation | 5-question configurator | form only |
| Fixed price on page | from-prices | hourly rate + estimates | configurator | full from-price board |
| Included/excluded lists | broad | base package | base package | per-offer, plus anti-services |

KluCode wins price transparency, honesty devices, privacy, accessibility and prose. It loses every proof row and every low-friction contact row. The wins are invisible until a visitor already trusts the site. The losses hit in the first thirty seconds.

## Prioritized actions

1. Fill hosting provider in `profile.ts` (C6, blocker, minutes).
2. Hide the `.crossroads-copy` scrollbar (C2, cosmetic part, minutes).
3. Screenshots of the three systems into Projekte and the lens (C1/C3, days, highest conversion impact).
4. Wire the evidence chain: offer to case, case to offer, FAQ to pages (J2, afternoon, free).
5. One testimonial with name and city per project, one number per project (C1, owner legwork).
6. Shorten the crossroads runway to about two viewports, and let proof precede the price board (C2/J1, day).
7. Portrait photo reshoot or tight crop (C5, owner legwork).
8. Regenerate logo and OG in Schibsted Grotesk (M1, hours).
9. Decide on the 4,500 to 5,500 € middle tier (C4, owner decision).
10. Self-scheduled 30-minute slot or WhatsApp (C7, owner decision).
11. One triage sentence for the undecided visitor on Leistungen and Kontakt (J3, minutes).
12. CI: clean install, build, `check:*` (M2, hours).
