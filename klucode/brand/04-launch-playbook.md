# KluCode — Launch Playbook

Pricing, the German admin setup, and a 90-day plan.

> **This is not tax or legal advice.** It is a researched checklist to walk into a
> *Steuerberater*'s office with, so that meeting costs you one hour instead of
> three. Every figure is current as of **August 2026** and needs confirming for
> your actual start year. Where a decision has money or liability attached, the
> checklist says *ask*.

---

## 1. Pricing

### 1.1 What the market pays

German freelance market data for 2026:

- All freelancers, average: **103 €/h**
- Web developers, average: **91 €/h**
- Frontend developers, average: **82 €/h**
- Typical band: junior 50–70 €/h · mid 70–100 €/h · senior with React/Node
  specialisation 100–150 €/h

Two things matter about 2026 specifically: the average rate **did not rise for
the first time since the surveys began**, so the market is tighter than it was —
and the spread is wide, which means positioning moves your rate far more than
experience does.

With a B.Sc., four years employed, and three delivered production systems, you
are **solidly mid**, with a full-stack claim most frontend freelancers cannot
make. That is worth being at the top of mid, not the bottom.

### 1.2 The rate card

| Line                              | Rate                    | Notes                                                          |
| --------------------------------- | ----------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Standard day rate** (8h)        | **680 €**               | The public "ab" anchor. ~85 €/h.                                |
| Agency / subcontract (segment B)  | **600–640 €**           | Discount buys volume and zero sales effort. Floor: 560 €.       |
| Corporate via platform (segment D)| **700–800 €**           | Platforms bear more risk and admin. Do not go below 650 €.      |
| Retainer maintenance              | **90–350 €/month**      | Tiered. Highest-margin line you have.                           |
| Emergency / same-week             | **+50%**                | Have the number ready before you are asked.                     |

**Never publish an hourly rate to segment A.** Owners hear an hourly rate and
start counting your hours instead of valuing the outcome.

### 1.3 Why fixed price, and how to not get burned

Fixed price is where your speed becomes *your* margin instead of a smaller
invoice. If a project takes you 6 days that an agency quotes 20 for, hourly
billing hands the entire benefit to the client and you earn less for being
better. That is backwards, and it is the core commercial reason the brand leads
with outcomes rather than hours.

The discipline that makes it safe:

1. **Scope in writing, always.** A one-page *Leistungsbeschreibung* listing what
   is included and — more important — **what is not**.
2. **Price at your realistic estimate + 30%.** The buffer is not padding; it is
   the premium the client pays to move the risk onto you. Say that out loud if
   asked; it is a completely respectable answer.
3. **Two revision rounds included, then hourly.** Written into the offer.
4. **Payment: 40% on signature, 30% at the usable version, 30% at handover.**
   Never start on 0%. The deposit is what separates a client from a browser.
5. **Change requests get a number and a new date, every time.** Politely,
   immediately, in writing. Scope creep is what kills fixed-price businesses, and
   it always arrives as a small favour.

### 1.4 What the day rate has to cover

At roughly **130 billable days a year** — realistic for a solo operator once you
subtract sales, admin, holiday, illness, and unbilled learning:

| Day rate | Gross revenue | Rough take-home after costs, health insurance, and tax |
| -------- | ------------- | ------------------------------------------------------ |
| 500 €    | 65.000 €      | ~34–38.000 €                                            |
| 600 €    | 78.000 €      | ~42–46.000 €                                            |
| **680 €**| **88.400 €**  | **~48–53.000 €**                                        |
| 750 €    | 97.500 €      | ~54–59.000 €                                            |

Directional only — your actual number depends on health insurance choice,
*Vorsorge*, and deductions. But the shape is the point: **130 days, not 220.**
The single most common freelance pricing error is dividing a target salary by
220 working days. That number is a fantasy and it prices you 40% too low.

## 2. German setup checklist

Roughly in order. Items marked **⚠** are the ones with real money or liability
attached.

### 2.1 Status — Freiberufler or Gewerbe

⚠ **This is the first and most consequential question.**

Software development can be *freiberuflich* under **§ 18 EStG** as an
*ingenieurähnlicher Beruf* — but only where the work is genuinely engineering-like
(systems analysis, architecture, individual software) **and** you hold comparable
qualifications in depth and breadth. **Your completed B.Sc. in Computer Science is
exactly the qualification that argument rests on**, and it is the reason this is
worth pursuing rather than defaulting to a trade registration.

Why it matters:

| | Freiberufler | Gewerbe |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Gewerbeanmeldung | not required | required |
| Gewerbesteuer | none | above the 24.500 € allowance |
| IHK membership + fees | none | mandatory |
| Accounting | simple **EÜR** | EÜR, plus *Bilanzierung* past thresholds |

Watch the boundary: building **individual software** for a client is the strong
case. Reselling licences, hosting as a packaged product, or affiliate income is
*gewerblich* — and mixing the two can infect your whole income
(*Abfärbetheorie*). If you plan to resell hosting, ask about keeping it in a
separate structure.

**Action:** submit the *Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung* via **ELSTER**
within a month of starting. Describe the activity in § 18 language — „Konzeption,
Entwicklung und Anpassung von Individualsoftware" — not „Webdesign", which invites
a *gewerblich* classification. The Finanzamt decides; get a Steuerberater to
phrase this one.

### 2.2 VAT — and why you should probably decline the small-business rule

The **Kleinunternehmerregelung (§ 19 UStG)** applies if prior-year turnover was
≤ **25.000 €** and current-year turnover stays ≤ **100.000 €** (a hard ceiling —
cross it and the status ends immediately, mid-year). In your founding year, the
25.000 € figure is the one to watch.

**⚠ You should almost certainly opt for *Regelbesteuerung* from day one.**

1. At 680 €/day you pass 25.000 € in **37 billable days** — likely inside your
   first five months. Starting as a *Kleinunternehmer* means changing your prices
   and re-papering your clients mid-year.
2. Your clients are businesses. They reclaim the VAT, so your 19% costs them
   **nothing**. The exemption only helps people selling to consumers.
3. You reclaim *Vorsteuer* on your own costs — laptop, monitor, software, phone,
   accountant. In a founding year that is a meaningful sum.

The exemption is designed for a different business than yours. Confirm with your
Steuerberater, but go in expecting to waive it.

### 2.3 Invoicing

- **§ 14 UStG mandatory fields** on every invoice: full name and address of both
  parties, your *Steuernummer* or USt-IdNr., invoice date, a unique sequential
  number, quantity and description of the service, date of supply, net amount, the
  VAT rate and amount, and any agreed reduction.
- Get a **USt-IdNr.** free from the BZSt. Use it instead of your *Steuernummer* on
  invoices — it does not expose your personal tax file.
- ⚠ **E-Rechnung:** since 1 January 2025 **every** German business must be able to
  **receive** structured e-invoices (XRechnung / ZUGFeRD). This already applies to
  you, from day one — a PDF mailbox is not sufficient. The obligation to *issue*
  them phases in for regular-taxed businesses through 2027/2028. Pick invoicing
  software that handles both formats now; retrofitting is annoying.
- Payment terms: **14 days net**. Not 30. You are one person, not a bank.

### 2.4 Insurance

- ⚠ **Berufshaftpflicht mit Vermögensschadendeckung** — the important one.
  Standard liability cover does *not* pay for pure financial loss, and pure
  financial loss is the only kind of damage a developer causes. If your commission
  calculation is wrong for six months, this is the policy that matters. Budget
  ~40–80 €/month; insist on IT-specific wording.
- **Krankenversicherung** — mandatory. Voluntary GKV or private. Model both
  honestly at 33: private is cheaper now and structurally more expensive later,
  and switching back is close to impossible.
- ⚠ **Rentenversicherung** — most self-employed developers are exempt, **but**
  § 2 Nr. 9 SGB VI makes *arbeitnehmerähnliche Selbständige* compulsorily insured:
  broadly, no employees **and** essentially one client. That is exactly the shape
  of a long segment-D platform contract. Check this before you sign a six-month
  full-time engagement, not after.
- **Berufsunfähigkeitsversicherung** — no employer safety net any more. Cheapest
  it will ever be at 33.

### 2.5 Scheinselbständigkeit

⚠ The single largest financial risk in German freelancing. If the *Deutsche
Rentenversicherung* reclassifies you as an employee, the client owes back social
contributions — up to four years, and up to 30 in cases of intent — and they will
come after you too. Contracts have been ended over this.

Warning signs, all of which describe a typical corporate contract:

- one client is more than ~5/6 of your income for a sustained period;
- fixed hours, a desk you must occupy, integration into their team hierarchy;
- a company email address, a company laptop, an entry in their org chart;
- no other clients, no own tooling, no ability to send a substitute.

**Mitigations:** keep at least two active clients; work remotely where possible;
use your own hardware and your own email; deliver against defined outcomes rather
than logged presence; keep your own website and marketing visibly running. This is
a further argument for keeping segments A and B alive even when a lucrative
segment-D contract is on the table.

### 2.6 DSGVO

- **AV-Vertrag (Art. 28 DSGVO)** with every client whose personal data you can
  reach — which is every CRM, POS, and contact form you will ever build. Have a
  template ready; producing one on request looks extremely professional.
- **TOMs** — a short written description of your technical and organisational
  measures. Two pages, once.
- **Verzeichnis von Verarbeitungstätigkeiten** for your own business.
- Your own site: no Google Fonts from the CDN, no Google Analytics without
  consent, no third-party embeds. The site in `web/` is built to need no cookie
  banner at all — see §3.3.

### 2.7 Contracts

Have three documents ready before your first pitch. A freelancer who produces a
proper contract in the first week is already ahead of most agencies.

1. **Rahmenvertrag / Werkvertrag** with the fixed-price terms from §1.3.
2. **AV-Vertrag** per §2.6.
3. **Leistungsbeschreibung** template — the one-page scope document.

⚠ Get the *Nutzungsrechte* clause right. „Der Code gehört Ihnen" is a brand
promise from `02-voice.md`, so the contract must actually grant it: an exclusive,
transferable, unlimited right of use on final payment — with a carve-out letting
you reuse generic components and libraries you wrote. Without that carve-out you
give away your own toolkit on every project.

## 3. The first 90 days

### Days 1–30 — foundation

- [ ] Name checks from `01-strategy.md` §1: domain, DPMA/EUIPO, handles, register
- [ ] Buy `klucode.de` (+ `.com` if free)
- [ ] Steuerberater — one hour, this document in hand
- [ ] ELSTER *Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung*
- [ ] Business bank account (separate from private — non-negotiable for sanity)
- [ ] Berufshaftpflicht in place **before** the first line of client code
- [ ] Ask all three past clients for a testimonial and a usable number
- [ ] Photograph: you, and screenshots of all three systems

### Days 31–60 — presence

- [ ] Deploy the website from `web/`
- [ ] Fill `profile.ts` and complete the Impressum — **the site must not go live
      without it**
- [ ] Have the Impressum and Datenschutzerklärung reviewed once (an IHK or a
      lawyer's flat-fee check — cheap insurance against a *Abmahnung*)
- [ ] LinkedIn: personal profile rewritten to the positioning, company page live
- [ ] Google Business Profile — „Softwareentwicklung Düsseldorf". Free, and it is
      how local SMBs actually find people
- [ ] Profiles on freelancermap and GULP (segment D floor)
- [ ] Business cards
- [ ] Contract templates from §2.7 drafted

### Days 61–90 — pipeline

- [ ] Personally contact all three past clients: you are now independent, ask
      about maintenance retainers (offer 4) — **this is your fastest revenue**
- [ ] Ask each for one referral. Specifically. By name if you can
- [ ] Ten Düsseldorf/NRW agencies contacted about subcontract capacity (segment B)
- [ ] One LinkedIn post per week, cadence per `02-voice.md` §6.3
- [ ] Publish the first proper case study, with a real number, client-approved
- [ ] Two local network events — *Digitale Stadt Düsseldorf*, IHK founder
      meet-ups, Rheinland dev meetups

### 3.1 Where the first paying work will actually come from

In this order. Effort should follow it:

1. **Your three existing clients.** They have paid you, they are satisfied, and
   they have not yet been asked for a retainer or a referral. Start here.
2. **Their referrals.** German SMBs hire on recommendation more than anything
   else.
3. **Agencies.** A single agency relationship can be 30% of a year's revenue.
4. **Local search and the website.** Slow to start, compounds.
5. **Platforms.** The floor, not the plan.

Cold outreach to strangers is last and lowest-yield. Do not start there because it
feels like work.

### 3.2 Numbers to watch

Enquiries per month · enquiry→call rate · call→offer rate · **offer→signature
rate** · average project value · retainer MRR · days billed per month.

If offer→signature is below ~30%, the problem is nearly always the offer document
or the price framing, not the lead quality.

### 3.3 Website launch checklist

- [ ] `profile.ts` complete — no placeholder left anywhere on the live site
- [ ] Impressum complete per § 5 DDG: full civil first and last name, a
      *ladungsfähige Anschrift* (a real street address — a P.O. box is not
      sufficient), email **plus** a second fast contact channel, and your
      USt-IdNr. once you have one
- [ ] Datenschutzerklärung matches what the site actually does
- [ ] No third-party requests at runtime — check the network tab with a cold cache
- [ ] No cookie banner needed, because no cookies are set. Verify, do not assume
- [ ] Contact route tested end to end; the mailto fallback works
- [ ] Lighthouse ≥ 95 across the board
- [ ] Keyboard-only pass through every page; visible focus everywhere
- [ ] `de` and `en` both render; `hreflang` correct
- [ ] OG card renders correctly in the LinkedIn Post Inspector
- [ ] `sitemap.xml` and `robots.txt` served
- [ ] Backup and a deployment you can repeat from a clean machine
