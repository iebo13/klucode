# KluCode — Brand Strategy

> The decisions this document makes are upstream of everything else in the kit.
> If the voice guide, the logo rules or the website copy ever contradict this
> file, this file wins.

---

## 1. The name

**KluCode** carries three readings at once, and they reinforce rather than
compete:

| Source                | Reading                                                   | What it promises                                          |
| --------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Klausmann**         | your own surname, shortened                                | A person stands behind this, not a shell company.          |
| German **klug**       | smart, considered, wise — not merely *clever*              | Code that was thought about before it was written.          |
| English **clue**      | the insight that unlocks the problem                        | Someone who works out what you actually need.               |

The surname is the one that makes the name defensible. A pun alone is a nice
coincidence; a pun built out of your own name is a **signature** — the same
gesture as *Bosch*, *Miele*, *Zeiss*, and it lands especially well with German
SMB owners, for whom a family name on the door is the oldest trust signal there
is. It also quietly answers the question every one-person business gets asked:
*who is actually behind this?*

And the layers stack in exactly the right order. The surname says **who**; *klug*
says **how**; *clue* says **what you get**. That hands you your entire
positioning: the value is not the typing, it is the **thinking that happens
before the typing**. Every piece of copy in this kit is downstream of that idea.

**Use the origin story — do not hide it.** On the About page, in a first call,
in the second line of a LinkedIn profile. It takes one sentence and it does more
work than a paragraph of adjectives:

> „KluCode — das *Klu* kommt von Klausmann. Und von *klug*, weil der Teil der
> Arbeit ist, der zählt."

One caution: because the mark is now partly a family name, the trademark check in
the next section matters *more*, not less. Check for existing marks from anyone
called Klausmann in software or IT services before you register anything.

Pronunciation to use out loud: *KLU-code* ("kloo"), not "K-L-U".

### Before you spend a euro on this name

Do these four checks first — in this order — and stop if any of them fails:

1. **Domain.** `klucode.de` is the one that matters for a Düsseldorf business;
   `klucode.com` is the nice-to-have. Check both at your registrar.
2. **Trademark.** Search the DPMA register (free, `register.dpma.de`) and the
   EUIPO eSearch for `KluCode` and near-misses (Klucode, KluKode, CluCode) in
   **Nice class 42** (software development) and class 35. You are not obliged to
   register a mark, but you must know you are not walking into someone else's.
3. **Handles.** LinkedIn company page, GitHub org, Instagram — grab them the same
   day you buy the domain, even if you will not use them all.
4. **Handelsregister / Unternehmensregister.** Check no German company already
   trades under a confusingly similar name in software.

Only after all four are clear should the logo files in this kit go anywhere near
a printer.

## 2. Positioning statement

> **KluCode builds the one system a business actually runs on — and builds it in
> weeks, not quarters.**
>
> For owner-run companies in the Rhineland who have outgrown spreadsheets, and
> for agencies and product teams who need frontend capacity that does not need
> managing. One engineer, end to end: interface, backend, database, server.

Say it shorter when you need to:

- **DE:** „Ich baue die Software, mit der Ihr Betrieb tatsächlich arbeitet — vom
  ersten Klick bis zum laufenden Server."
- **EN:** "I build the system your business actually runs on — front end to
  server, one person, start to finish."

### The tagline

**„Klug gebaut."** (DE) — two words, owns the name, and it is a claim about
judgement rather than speed or price. Both of those are easy to undercut;
judgement is not.

**"Clever, not complicated."** (EN) — the English line does not translate the
German one, because *klug* has no good one-word English equivalent. It carries
the same idea: the intelligence shows up as *less*, not as more.

Never use both lines in the same lockup.

## 3. The insight the brand is built on

Small and mid-sized German companies are not underserved by software. They are
served *badly*, from two directions at once:

- **Agencies** quote €30–60k and four months for something whose real scope is
  three weeks. The client pays for account managers, briefing loops, and the
  handover losses between the person who understood the problem and the person
  who wrote the code.
- **Cheap freelancers and website builders** deliver a shell that looks fine and
  collapses the moment the business needs it to do something specific — a
  commission calculation, a shift-aware till, a comparison logic.

So the owner keeps running the business out of Excel and WhatsApp, because both
available options are wrong.

**KluCode is the third option**: one qualified engineer who talks to the owner
directly, understands the operation, and ships the whole system — at a fixed
price the owner agrees to before anything starts.

That is not a compromise between the two. It is a genuinely different product,
and the reason it is now possible is section 6.

## 4. Who KluCode sells to

You chose to keep all four doors open. That is defensible — but only if they are
ordered, because they want different things and a brand that shouts at all four
equally will convince none of them. Priority order:

### A. Owner-run SMBs in the Rhineland — *the core, ~60% of effort*

Handwerk, Gastronomie, Dienstleister, Vertriebsorganisationen, Kanzleien.
5–50 employees. The decision-maker is the owner; there is no IT department and no
procurement process.

- **Buys:** relief from a manual process that is costing them hours or errors.
- **Fears:** being locked in, being talked down to, paying for something that is
  never finished.
- **Decides on:** trust and clarity, not on your tech stack.
- **This is where your three existing projects came from.** It is proven demand.

### B. Agencies as a subcontractor — *steady base load, ~20%*

Düsseldorf/Cologne agencies needing React/Next.js capacity that does not need
hand-holding.

- **Buys:** reliability and a developer who can be dropped into an existing
  codebase and a client call without embarrassing them.
- **Speak to them in:** day rates, availability windows, stack specifics.

### C. Product teams and startups — *opportunistic, ~15%*

Remote, English-speaking, React/Next.js feature work.

- **Buys:** velocity on a defined slice of a product.
- **This is what the `/en` side of the website is for.**

### D. Corporates via freelance platforms — *the cash floor, ~5%*

freelancermap, GULP, Hays, Westhouse contracts in NRW.

- **Buys:** a body with a matching skills matrix. Brand barely matters here; your
  profile and rate do.
- **Handle with care:** long full-time on-site contracts with a single client
  create **Scheinselbständigkeit** exposure. See `04-launch-playbook.md`.

> **The tension to manage:** A and D pull the brand in opposite directions —
> A wants a trusted local partner, D wants an interchangeable resource.
> Resolve it by never letting D touch the brand. Segment D is served by your
> **platform profiles**, not by klucode.de. The website speaks to A, with a clear
> door for B and C.

## 5. The offer architecture

Four products, deliberately few. Prices are the *public* anchors — the reasoning
behind them is in `04-launch-playbook.md`.

| #   | Offer                     | For  | Shape                | Public anchor            |
| --- | ------------------------- | ---- | -------------------- | ------------------------ |
| 1   | **Website & Landingpage** | A    | Fixed price, 2–3 wks | ab **2.500 €**           |
| 2   | **Individuelle Web-App**  | A    | Fixed price, 4–10 wks | ab **9.000 €**          |
| 3   | **Entwickler-Kapazität**  | B, C | Day rate, min. 5 days | ab **680 €/Tag**        |
| 4   | **Betrieb & Wartung**     | all  | Monthly retainer     | ab **90 €/Monat**        |

Offer 2 is the one that matches your largest delivered project and carries the
best margin. Offer 1 is the door people walk in through. Offer 4 is what turns a
project business into something with a floor under it — **push it on every
single project**, because three retainers cover your rent whether or not you sell
anything that month.

**Always quote fixed price to segment A.** Owners do not want to buy hours; they
want to buy an outcome and know the number. Fixed price is also where your speed
converts into margin instead of into a smaller invoice — which is the entire
commercial logic of section 6.

## 6. The differentiator, and how to talk about it

You built three production systems, solo, using AI-assisted development. That is
your actual edge, and it is why the fixed-price maths works: you deliver an
agency-scale scope at a one-person cost base.

**How to position it — outcome first, method second.**

The headline is always the outcome: *fixed price, weeks not months, one person
who owns it end to end.* The method is explained honestly on the
`/ansatz` (`/approach`) page, one click away, for the people who ask.

Why this order, and not the other one:

- Segment A does not buy "AI-powered". They buy "done in three weeks for a price
  you agreed in advance". Leading with AI invites the objection *"so I'm paying
  you to type into a chatbot?"* before you have established any value.
- But hiding it entirely would be worse. It is verifiable, it is the truthful
  answer to "how are you this fast?", and being open about it reads as
  confidence. Concealing it reads as something to hide.

**The objection, and the answer.** Put this on the approach page more or less
verbatim, because you will be asked it in every second sales call:

> **„Schreibt das nicht einfach eine KI?"**
>
> Nein — eine KI tippt schneller, als ich tippen kann. Was sie nicht kann:
> entscheiden, was gebaut werden soll, wo die Grenzen Ihres Geschäfts liegen und
> welche Fehler Sie am Monatsende Geld kosten. Das ist meine Arbeit. Ich prüfe,
> teste und verantworte jede Zeile, die ausgeliefert wird — mit einem
> abgeschlossenen Informatikstudium und vier Jahren Berufserfahrung im Rücken.
> Der Unterschied für Sie ist nicht die Qualität. Es ist der Preis und der
> Termin.

That paragraph does three jobs at once: it concedes the premise, it relocates the
value to judgement (which is the *name*), and it closes on the client's benefit.

### The other three differentiators

1. **One person, no relay.** The person in the meeting is the person writing the
   code. Nothing is lost in a handover, because there is no handover.
2. **The whole system, not just the surface.** Frontend, backend, database,
   deployment, server. Evidenced by the CRM project: React, Node.js, Express,
   PostgreSQL, on a Plesk server you administered yourself.
3. **Formally qualified.** A B.Sc. in Computer Science is not a small thing in
   Germany. It matters commercially — it is the basis of your *Freiberufler*
   status — and it matters in the room, because it separates you from the large
   population of self-taught web builders your prospects have been burned by.

## 7. Proof — the three projects

These are the whole portfolio. Presented properly, three real systems beat twenty
templates. Client names stay out unless you have written permission; the sector
and the numbers do the work.

| Project                                                             | Framed as                                                         | The line that sells it                                                                 |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **CRM + Provisionsverwaltung + Vergleichsportal** (React, Node, Express, PostgreSQL, Plesk) | "Drei Systeme, die vorher drei Excel-Dateien waren." | Commission settlement that used to be manual, now derived automatically from the data. |
| **Kassensystem für eine Shisha-Bar** (Frontend + Datenbank)         | "Ein Kassensystem, das zum Betrieb passt — nicht umgekehrt."       | Built around how the venue actually runs a shift.                                       |
| **Landingpage für eine Reinigungsfirma** (Vanilla JS)               | "Kein Baukasten, kein Framework-Ballast. Eine Seite, die lädt."    | Plus the LinkedIn campaign — you did not just build it, you brought it traffic.         |

**Do not undersell the cleaning-company project.** A landing page *plus* an
AI-produced LinkedIn video campaign is not a small web job — it is a complete
customer-acquisition package, and it is the only piece of evidence you have that
you think past the code to whether the client actually gets customers. For
segment A that is often the more persuasive story.

**Before the site goes live**, collect from each client:

- a two-sentence written testimonial (offer to draft it for them — they will
  almost always accept),
- permission to name them, or a fallback description ("Vertriebsorganisation aus
  NRW, 20 Mitarbeiter"),
- one concrete number: hours saved per week, error rate, time to launch.

One real number outperforms every adjective on the page.

## 8. Personality

KluCode sounds like **a good engineer explaining something to the owner of a
business, as an equal**. Calm, precise, direct, unhurried. Never salesy, never
performatively casual, never mystifying.

Archetype: **the Craftsman**, with a strong streak of the **Sage**. The pride is
in the work being right, and in the client understanding why.

Five adjectives, in priority order:

**klar** · **ruhig** · **direkt** · **gründlich** · **unaufgeregt**

### What KluCode is not

- Not an agency, and does not pretend to have a team. "Wir" is a lie your first
  Zoom call exposes. **KluCode says "ich".**
- Not a growth-hacker. No urgency theatre, no "nur noch 2 Slots frei".
- Not a hype merchant. No "revolutionär", no "disruptiv", no rocket emoji.
- Not cheap. Cheap is a segment you cannot win and should not want.
- Not a generalist. It does not do print design, SEO retainers, or social media
  management. It builds systems.

## 9. What "winning" looks like in year one

The brand exists to serve these; if an idea does not move one of them, it is
decoration.

1. **Three retainers on the books** (offer 4) — a revenue floor before any new
   sale.
2. **Two projects that came in through the website**, not through the personal
   network. That is the test of whether the brand is doing any work at all.
3. **One agency relationship** (segment B) delivering predictable base load.
4. **A published case study with a real number in it**, cleared by the client.
