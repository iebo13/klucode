# KluCode visual audit, 26 August 2026

**Scope:** style, layout, aesthetic. Not copy, not SEO, not performance, except
where those decide what the page looks like.

**Audited:** https://iebo13.github.io/klucode/de/ and the five sub-pages, in a
real Chromium via Playwright at 1440x900, 1280x800 and 390x844, in both colour
schemes. Numbers below were measured in the live DOM, not estimated from
screenshots. The source in `web/src` and `brand/tokens` was read alongside.
Comparison sites in section 8 were opened and screenshotted the same day.

**Relationship to the 25 August audit:** that one asked whether the site sells.
This one asks whether it looks like the work of the person it claims to be. The
structural fixes from the 25th landed. The homepage is 5,446 px instead of
8,564, the crossroads is unpinned, the draft offer is gone. What follows is
what is left once those are true.

The stance is critical by request. Where something is good it gets one line.

> **Status, same day.** Every item in section 9 was worked into the site on
> 26 August 2026, on branch `claude/visual-audit-2026-08-26`. Section 11 at the
> foot of this file records what each one became, including the two the
> measurements refused. Four owner decisions were put to the owner before any
> code was written and their answers are recorded there too.

## 1. Verdict in one screen

The craft is in the code and it has not reached the surface. Every token is
reasoned, every comment defends a measurement, and the result on screen is a
tall dark column of well set type with nothing to look at. The site does not
look cheap. It looks **unfinished in the places where design is supposed to
show**: the compositions are half empty, the page has one hue and three
near-identical darks, and on a phone it renders **zero images across the entire
homepage**.

The single sentence version: this is a beautifully built wireframe.

Ten findings, ranked by how much they cost the first impression.

| # | Finding | Severity | Where |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | On a 390 px phone the homepage is 6,750 px tall and contains **not one image**. The 3D scene does not mount, and its poster is `hidden` below 640 px. The only picture on the whole site is a portrait on `/ueber-mich`. | Critical | Site-wide |
| 2 | The hero spends a full viewport on type and leaves the right 390 px of its own container empty. The competitor sitting one search result away puts a proof artefact in exactly that slot. | Critical | Home |
| 3 | The page has **four different left edges**: 176 px, 189 px, 32 px, 176 px. The 32 px one is a deliberate `bleed` exception, but at 1280 px it reads as a 64 px misalignment rather than as punctuation. | High | Home |
| 4 | In dark mode the `tint` section and the plain section differ by **0.4 L\* units**. The site's alternation device does nothing for the readers who see it in dark. | High | Site-wide |
| 5 | The 3D scene at rest is its emptiest shot. Four toy objects in a 785x900 void, no affordance saying that hovering does anything. Most desktop readers never see the good frames. | High | Home |
| 6 | The type ramp collapses at the bottom: card titles are 24 px and the paragraph under them is 22 px. A two pixel step is not a hierarchy. | High | Site-wide |
| 7 | Form fields measure **1.26:1** against the page and their border **1.41:1** against their own fill. WCAG 1.4.11 asks 3:1 for a control boundary. The fields have effectively no edge. | High | Kontakt |
| 8 | The FAQ column is 768 px inside a 1088 px container and the closing CTA leaves its right half empty apart from one orphaned pill. Roughly a third of the lower page is unclaimed air. | Medium | Home |
| 9 | The system diagram is 12 px mono in a 560 unit viewBox. On a phone it renders 293 px wide, so the labels are about 6.3 px, and the third label sits **on top of its own curve** at every size. | Medium | Home, Projekte |
| 10 | The portrait is an untreated grey-studio headshot. It is the brightest rectangle on the site and it belongs to a different visual world. | Medium | Über mich |

What is genuinely good and should not be touched: the type choice and its
setting at display size, the tracking on the H1, the price rows in the services
board, the `Vorher / Nachher / Ergebnis` blocks on `/projekte`, the
`Kurz und sachlich` spec list, the grain on the ink slabs, and the fact that
nothing on the page moves without being asked.

## 3. The page as a composition

### 3.1 Four left edges

The homepage has a spine at 176 px. Three of six sections sit on it. The
services panel sits at 189. The work section sits at 32.

The 32 is deliberate. `Section` has a `bleed` prop and its comment is explicit:
one structural exception, because seven sections sharing one left edge made the
page read as a single undifferentiated column. The reasoning is right. The
execution is width-dependent in a way that undoes it:

| Viewport | Spine | Bleed edge | Offset |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1280 | 96 | 32 | 64 px |
| 1440 | 176 | 32 | 144 px |
| 1920 | 384 | 128 | 256 px |

At 1920 the exception reads as a deliberate wide moment. At 1280, which is the
single most common desktop viewport, a 64 px offset reads as a bug. A device
that only works above a certain width is a device that fails for most of the
audience.

The 189 px on the services panel is not deliberate at all. It is the container
left edge plus the panel's own 24 px padding, minus the panel's negative row
margin. Nobody chose it.

**Fix:** keep exactly one exception and make it unmistakable. Run the work
section edge to edge (0 px, not 32) with the cards themselves inset, or drop
the bleed and give the section a different ground instead. Half a bleed is
worse than none. Pull the services panel onto the spine by moving its padding
inward so the eyebrow starts at 176 like every other eyebrow on the page.

### 3.2 The empty right column

Three sections leave a large unclaimed area on the right, and in each case it is
the area the eye lands on after reading the heading.

- **Hero.** Longest H1 line ends at x=1051. The container ends at 1264. The
  viewport ends at 1440. That is 389 px of nothing to the right of the loudest
  thing on the page.
- **FAQ.** The accordion is 768 px wide in a 1088 px container. 320 px of the
  container is empty for the section's full 812 px height.
- **Closing CTA.** Heading, lead and button all live in the left 620 px. The
  right half holds one pill, `Freie Kapazität ab 2027`, floated bottom-right at
  x=923 and aligned to nothing.

Whitespace that surrounds content is composition. Whitespace that sits beside
content because nothing was put there is a hole. These are holes. You can tell
which is which by asking whether removing the container cap would change
anything, and here it would not, because the ink already stops well short of it.

**Fix:** put something in the slot or close the slot.
- Hero: a proof artefact (see 8.2).
- FAQ: either narrow the container to `containerNarrow` (48 rem) and centre it,
  which makes the emptiness symmetric and intentional, or put the section
  heading in the right column and let the questions run full width.
- CTA: the capacity pill belongs directly under the button as a status line, or
  in the header where availability actually informs a decision. Where it is now
  it looks like a stray element.

### 3.3 One rhythm for six sections

Five of six sections use `py-section` and land on exactly 104 px top and 104 px
bottom at this viewport. A 645 px closing CTA and a 1,102 px work grid are
framed identically.

The consequence is visible at the bottom of the Ablauf section: the left column
ends at y=390, the right column at y=330, and then there is 110 px of nothing
before the boundary. The section does not end, it runs out.

Uniform rhythm is the right default and the wrong final answer. A page needs
somewhere to breathe more and somewhere to compress, or every section carries
the same weight and the reader has no way to tell which one matters.

**Fix:** two values, not one. Keep 104 px for the workhorse sections and give
the hero and the closing CTA a larger one (roughly 160 px), so the ink bookends
read as chapter breaks. Then let sections whose columns finish early pull their
bottom padding in, so the section boundary lands close to the last line of ink.

### 3.4 The hero gives away its best real estate

The hero is 716 px in a 900 px viewport. The H1 is set at 96 px over three lines
whose widths are 756, 474 and 875 px in a 1088 px measure. `text-wrap: balance`
produced that, and the middle line is 614 px short of the column.

The result is a display setting that never reaches an edge. Compare the same
treatment on Linear, where the H1 spans about 78 percent of the container on
both lines and the lead below it is deliberately quiet at small size. Here the
lead is 22 px over three lines and 888 px wide, which is longer than two of the
three headline lines. The subordinate element out-measures the dominant one.

**Fix:** three changes, all cheap.
1. Turn `text-wrap: balance` off on the H1 and hard-break the line yourself, or
   narrow the H1 measure to about 900 px so balance has less to work with. A
   two line H1 at this size will nearly always beat a three line one.
2. Drop the hero lead from `lead` (22 px) to `body` (17 px) and cap it near
   50ch. It is context, not a second headline.
3. Fill the right of the hero. See 6.1 and 8.2.

## 4. Colour and light

### 4.1 Three near identical darks

The dark palette resolves to three greens that a reader cannot tell apart.

| Role | Hex | L\* |
|---|---|---|
| `surface` (the page) | `#1C201C` | 11.7 |
| `surfaceAlt` (the tint) | `#112413` | 12.1 |
| `inkSurface` and `surfaceRaised` | `#2D322D` | 20.2 |

Two consequences, both bad.

**The tint device does nothing.** `surfaceAlt` differs from `surface` by 0.4 L\*
units. The Ablauf section is supposed to be visibly a different band, and in the
dark theme it is the same value with a slight hue shift that a reader perceives
as, at most, an uneven backlight. In the light theme the same pair is 93.7
against 94.0, also identical in value, but there a hue shift on a bright ground
does register, so the mint band works. **The site's alternation device is
functional in light and inert in dark**, which is the theme most developers and
a large share of visitors will see.

**Ink is lighter than the page.** In dark mode `inkSurface` resolves to
stone-900, which is one step *lighter* than `surface` at stone-950. So the hero,
the closing CTA and the footer are pale bands and the middle of the page is the
dark part. That is the opposite of the intent stated in the `Section` comment,
where ink is described as the peak surface the page is framed in. In light mode
the same frame is a 82 L\* drop and it is genuinely dramatic. The best
compositional idea in the design is neutralised for half the audience.

`surfaceRaised` also resolves to stone-900, which means **a card sitting on a
paper section is exactly the same colour as the hero**. There are three surface
roles and two distinct values.

**Fix:** stop borrowing from the same six-step neutral for three different jobs.
In the dark scheme set the page to the darkest value, ink one step darker still
or, better, to a distinctly cooler and deeper green, and raised panels clearly
above the page. Concretely: page around L\* 12, raised around L\* 18, ink around
L\* 7. Then give `surfaceAlt` a real value separation from the page, at least
4 L\* units, rather than a hue-only one. Two of these are single token edits in
`brand/tokens/tokens.json`.

### 4.2 One hue, and it is not enough

The palette is viridian plus a stone scale that is itself green-tinted. There is
a warm accent (`warm-300` / `warm-600`) and across the whole homepage it appears
exactly once, as the dot inside the capacity pill.

Monochrome is a legitimate 2026 direction, and the research supports it: single
hue pushed to extremes is a live trend. But monochrome works by pushing the one
hue hard, which means the green needs to appear at full saturation somewhere,
at scale, as a field. Here it never does. `viridian-500` is explicitly reserved
as a display colour for the mark alone, and everything else uses 600 or 300 for
contrast reasons. So the brand colour is present only as small text, thin
rules, 8 px dots and one button.

The site is not monochrome. It is desaturated.

**Fix:** pick one place per page where the green becomes the ground rather than
the ink. The obvious candidate is the closing CTA: a full viridian slab with the
heading knocked out of it would give the page a peak it currently lacks and
would make the button that lives there mean something. The contrast rules
already give you `onBrand` for exactly this. A second candidate is the eyebrow
rule or the section number chips, set as filled rather than outlined.

### 4.3 Borders that do not exist

Measured, dark theme:

- Form field fill against the page: **1.26:1**
- Form field border against its own fill: **1.41:1**
- Panel border against the page: **1.78:1**

WCAG 1.4.11 asks for 3:1 on the boundary of a control. The text roles on this
site were checked against a script and pass comfortably (muted ink text lands at
7.5:1). The non-text boundaries were not, and they fail.

The visual result on `/kontakt` is four input boxes that read as slightly
different patches of the same darkness. The textarea only reads as a field
because it is tall and has a resize grip.

**Fix:** raise `--kc-border` in the dark scheme to at least stone-700 for
controls, and give fields a fill that steps clearly away from the page rather
than sitting 1.26:1 from it. Extend `check_contrast.py` to assert 3:1 on
`border`, `navBorder` and the field roles, so this cannot regress. This is the
one finding in the audit that is both an accessibility failure and the reason a
whole page looks unfinished.

### 4.4 The half-converted light theme

Forcing light gives you: a light nav capsule over a dark hero, a dark services
section, then light content, then a dark closing CTA. That is by design, since
ink is dark in both schemes, and as a device the ink frame is good.

But the toggle then controls only the middle three sections, and the first thing
a reader sees after pressing it is a bright capsule sitting on an unchanged dark
hero, which reads as a partial failure rather than as a frame. Meanwhile the
light theme is, section for section, the better looking of the two: white cards
on a soft green wash have depth that the dark theme's flat near-blacks do not.

**Fix:** decide which theme is the site. If dark is the default, fix 4.1 so the
dark theme has the depth the light one already has. If the light theme is
better, and on the evidence of `/projekte` it is, then make light the default
and keep the ink bookends as the accent. Either way, the toggle should not be
the second thing in the nav. It is developer chrome in the most valuable strip
of the page.

## 5. Typography

The type is the strongest part of the design and it has three specific faults.

### 5.1 The ramp collapses at the bottom

| Role | Size | Weight |
|---|---|---|
| display / h1 | 96 px | 700 |
| h2 | 40 px | 700 |
| h3 | 24 px | 500 |
| lead | 22 px | 400 |
| body | 17 px | 400 |
| small | 14 px | 500 |

96 to 40 is a 2.4x step. 40 to 24 is 1.7x. **24 to 22 is 1.09x.**

On `/projekte` and on the homepage work cards, the project title is 24 px and
the description directly beneath it is 22 px. The face and weight differ, which
is why it does not collapse entirely, but at a glance the card has no clear
head. The `h3 { font-weight: 500 }` change in `globals.css` was made for exactly
this reason and it treated the symptom. The cause is that `lead` sits one
notch below `h3` on the same scale.

**Fix:** either raise h3 to about 28 px, or stop using `lead` inside cards and
use `body` there. The second is one class change and is probably right anyway,
since a 22 px paragraph inside a card is oversized for its measure.

### 5.2 Narrow columns holding wide type

`Warum das in Wochen geht und nicht in Quartalen.` is a 40 px heading set in a
341 px column. It breaks into five lines:

```
Warum das
in Wochen
geht und nicht
in Quartalen.
```

Four of those lines end at wildly different points. A 40 px display face wants
roughly 12 to 18 characters per line at minimum, and this column gives it about
9. The same section puts the body copy in the wider 624 px column, so the layout
has it backwards: the narrow column holds the type that needs width.

**Fix:** swap the columns, or widen the heading column to about 480 px, which
takes the heading to three lines with a much steadier rag.

### 5.3 Right-aligned notes with a ragged left

In the services board, the price note column is `text-align: right` with a
`max-width: 9rem`. `pro Tag · Rahmenvertrag möglich` wraps to three lines:

```
        pro Tag ·
   Rahmenvertrag
          möglich
```

Right-aligned text produces a ragged left edge, which is the harder rag to read
and the one that looks accidental. The CSS comment defends the two line case.
The three line case was not anticipated and it is the worst looking row in the
section.

**Fix:** left-align the note under the price and let the price stay right
aligned, or shorten the note. Right alignment is fine for a single line and
degrades badly past two.

### 5.4 What is right

The H1 tracking at -0.035em and a line height of exactly 1.0 is a confident,
correct display setting. Schibsted Grotesk at 700 holds up at 96 px. The mono
tags on the project cards, the `01` to `04` numerals and the diagram labels are
the one place mono earns itself. `hyphens: manual` on the service names is the
kind of detail almost nobody fixes. None of this needs changing.

## 6. Imagery, or the absence of it

### 6.1 The homepage has no pictures

At 390x844 the homepage is 6,750 px tall and contains:

- 1 `<img>`, `crossroads.webp`, rendered at **0x0** because it carries
  `hidden ... sm:block` and 390 is below the `sm` breakpoint
- 1 SVG logo, 41x29
- 1 SVG system diagram, 293x125
- 0 canvases, because the scene needs 1024 px width and 736 px height

So the picture count on the device most visitors use is zero. The whole homepage
is type, hairlines and one illegible line drawing.

At 1440 it is better, because the 3D scene mounts, but the count is still: one
scene, one diagram, nothing else. There is no screenshot of anything you built
anywhere on the site. `/projekte` describes three shipped systems in careful
prose and shows none of them.

This is the finding that matters most for aesthetics, and it is not really a
design problem. It is an assets problem that has been left open since the last
audit. A designer cannot compose a page out of nothing.

**Fix, in order of cost:**
1. Three product screenshots, one per project, cropped to a single screen that
   shows the moment of value. Anonymise the data. Frame them in a plain dark
   browser chrome so they sit in the palette. This alone changes `/projekte`
   from a text page into a portfolio.
2. One of those three, or a composite, in the hero's empty right column.
3. A phone-shaped crop of the crossroads poster so the mobile services section
   has a picture, or hide the poster properly at every size and give that
   section a different visual instead.

### 6.2 The 3D scene at rest

Unpinning the crossroads was right, and the hover-driven camera is a better idea
than the scroll-driven one. Two problems remain, and they are aesthetic rather
than structural.

**The default frame is the worst frame.** With no pointer on the board, the
camera sits at the junction. At 1440x900 that puts four small objects in a
horizontal band roughly 100 px tall, centred in a 785x900 region, with about
250 px of empty dark above and 400 px below. The four labels float at four
unrelated heights, and `04 Betrieb & Wartung` sits near the top right while its
object sits at the bottom right, so the pill and the thing it names are 350 px
apart with nothing connecting them.

Hover row 02 and the scene becomes good: the dashboard mock is legible, credible
and specific, and the shot is well composed against the free region. That is the
frame the section should be selling. Almost nobody will see it, because nothing
on the page suggests the rows do anything. The rows are links, they have a hover
fill, and there is no cursor change, no caption, no hint.

**The objects are two grades of quality in one shot.** The two monitors, with
their mock landing page and mock dashboard, are genuinely good and specific. The
database cylinder reads as a beige tub, the server rack reads as a grey filing
cabinet, and the office is three orange desks with blue-headed figures. In the
close-up at 1280 the good screen and the clip-art props are in the same frame
and the props win, because a viewer grades a scene by its weakest object.

**Fix:**
1. Give the scene a default frame worth looking at. Either open on the 02
   close-up rather than the junction, or move the junction camera in enough that
   the objects fill the vertical middle of the free region instead of a 100 px
   band in it.
2. Add one line of affordance. A `Fahren Sie über eine Zeile` note under the
   board, or a leader line from each pill to its object, or simply a
   `cursor: pointer` and a visible arrow on the focused row.
3. Cut the props. Four monitors showing four different real screens would be a
   stronger and more honest scene than four metaphors, and it would reuse the
   screenshots from 6.1. If the office and the cloud stay, they need the same
   fidelity as the dashboard or they need to become abstract, not stay halfway.

### 6.3 The system diagram

The `SystemDiagram` viewBox is 560x240 with 12 px mono labels and no responsive
handling.

- On a phone it renders 293 px wide, so the labels are about **6.3 CSS px**.
  They are not readable, and on a card that leads with `Drei Systeme, eine
  Datenbank` the drawing that proves it is noise.
- The third source label collides with its own curve. `Vergleichsportal` sits at
  x=34 with a baseline at y=186, and the cubic from (20,196) to (288,120) passes
  through y=184 at x=103. The line crosses the word at every viewport. It is
  visible in both the desktop and mobile screenshots.
- On mobile the diagram is placed *below* the `Projekte im Detail` link, so the
  card ends on a picture the reader has already been invited to leave.

**Fix:** move the third label above its node rather than beside it, or shift the
label anchors to x=34 with the baseline at `y - 16` for the diverging rows. Add
a second, taller viewBox for narrow widths (stacked, 14 px labels), or hide the
diagram below `sm` the way the crossroads poster is hidden. Move it above the
link in the card's source order.

### 6.4 The portrait

The headshot on `/ueber-mich` is a light grey studio backdrop with neutral
lighting. It is the brightest rectangle on the entire site and it is 82 L\*
units away from everything around it. It is a good photograph and it belongs to
a different design.

**Fix:** it needs a treatment, not a replacement. Cheapest credible option is a
duotone or a graded version pushed toward the viridian ground, which is a
standard move and would make the picture feel like part of the site. Second
option is to re-shoot against a dark ground. Third, and weakest, is to keep it
and give it a heavier frame so the jump reads as deliberate.

This portrait also still does not appear on the homepage, which the previous
audit flagged and which remains the strongest available trust device.

## 7. Component and detail craft

### 7.1 The floating capsule and what slides under it

The header is a 69 px capsule, `position: fixed`, inside a transparent
full-width bar. The capsule itself is well made: 0.86 fill, real border, blur
with saturation, one shadow, no sheen. The contrast reasoning behind the 0.86 is
sound.

The problem is the transparent bar around it. Content scrolls under the top edge
of the viewport with nothing over it, so at most scroll positions there is a
sliced line of text visible above and beside the capsule. Screenshots at y=2,300
and y=3,700 both show a half-cut heading sitting to the left of the pill. The
capsule looks like it is floating on the page. The clipped text looks like a
rendering fault.

**Fix:** either add a short scrim gradient across the full width behind the
capsule, fading from the surface colour to transparent over about 90 px, or
shrink the capsule on scroll so it stops competing. The first is one gradient
and solves it everywhere.

### 7.2 The mobile drawer

The drawer is a translucent panel over the hero. The blur is too weak to hide
what is behind it: `Software, mit der Ihr Betrieb tatsächlich arbeitet` is
plainly readable through the menu items. There is no scrim over the rest of the
page, so the drawer reads as a card that happens to be on top rather than as a
mode.

The footer of the drawer pairs `Switch to English` as text with the theme toggle
as an unlabelled sun icon in a circle. Two controls of the same rank, presented
two different ways.

**Fix:** raise the drawer fill toward opaque and add a dimming scrim behind it.
Label the theme control, or drop it from the drawer.

### 7.3 Form fields

Covered in 4.3. Additionally, the consent checkbox is 16 px, which is small for
the one control that gates submission, and the form sits directly on the page
with no panel, so the two columns of `/kontakt` have no visual boundary between
`Direkt` and `Oder hier`.

**Fix:** the border fix from 4.3, a 20 px checkbox, and either a `.panel` around
the form or a vertical hairline between the columns.

### 7.4 The orphan pill

`Freie Kapazität ab 2027` floats at the bottom right of the closing CTA,
aligned to nothing, at a vertical position between the button and the email
link. It is the only element on that side of the section.

Worth noting separately: today is August 2026, so the pill says the next
available slot is at least four months out. That is a content decision, not a
design one, but visually it means the loudest isolated element in the closing
section is a reason not to get in touch.

### 7.5 The footer

505 px tall. Content occupies the outer 141 px and 220 px of an 1088 px
container, with 727 px of air between. The two link columns hold five items and
three, so they do not balance each other either. There is no address, no phone,
no map, no social link, which for a German local business is a missed trust
block as well as a compositional one.

**Fix:** three or four columns of roughly equal weight, with the NAP details
(name, address, phone) as a real column. That fills the hole and adds the
signal at the same time.

### 7.6 The hidden scrollbar

`scrollbar-width: none` on `html`, site-wide, with a comment acknowledging the
trade-off and marking it as the owner's call. It is your call, and it is worth
restating the cost in visual terms: on a 6,750 px mobile page with no images and
uniform section padding, the scrollbar was the only remaining signal of position
and progress. Removing it makes a long page feel longer. If it stays, the
section rhythm has to do that job, which is another reason to fix 3.3 and 4.1.

## 8. How others do this

Three comparisons, all opened and looked at rather than recalled.

### 8.1 The direct German competitor

[codehero.solutions/webagentur](https://www.codehero.solutions/webagentur) sells
the same thing to the same buyer: Next.js and React at a fixed price for the
Mittelstand. Their hero is heading on the left and, on the right, **a mocked
browser window showing a Lighthouse audit at 100 / 100 / 100 / 100**, then two
CTAs, then credential chips (AWS Solutions Architect, Stripe), then a metrics
strip below the fold.

That is exactly the slot KluCode leaves empty. And the irony is sharp: KluCode
actually measures 97 / 100 / 100 on Lighthouse, has no third-party requests, and
never shows any of it. A competitor is winning the hero with a screenshot of a
number that this site could show truthfully.

[siondesign.de](https://siondesign.de/webdesign-duesseldorf), the Düsseldorf
competitor, opens on a star rating from 49 verified reviews, then a client logo
row, then a featured case study with full-width screenshots, then a team
photograph. Fourteen sections, and the visual density is highest exactly where
the buyer is deciding.

Neither is better designed than KluCode. Both are more convincing to look at,
because both show things.

### 8.2 Dark, type-led, one accent

[linear.app](https://linear.app) is the closest reference for what this site is
trying to be: near-black ground, one accent, a grotesk display face, no
decoration. Three things it does that KluCode does not.

1. **The H1 fills its measure.** Two lines, both reaching about 78 percent of
   the container. No ragged 474 px orphan line.
2. **The lead is quiet.** Small, muted, one line. It never competes with the
   headline the way a 22 px three-line lead does.
3. **The fold is committed to a product screenshot** that starts above the fold
   and continues past it. The type sells the idea and the picture proves it
   exists.

Its nav is also a full-width bar with a hairline, not a floating capsule, so
nothing ever slides visibly under it.

### 8.3 What the field says about 3D and about heroes

Two findings from the current literature that bear directly on decisions already
made here.

On 3D: the consensus is that **a single well-lit hero object plus a reveal on
interaction** is enough to reposition a site as premium, and that you do not
need an explorable world, just one confident centrepiece. That is an argument
for cutting the crossroads down to the dashboard shot rather than for keeping
four objects of uneven quality. The same sources are explicit that hover-based
reveals need a discoverable affordance, usually a cursor change or an on-canvas
hint, which this scene has neither of.

On heroes: for anything sold on what it does, a screenshot or a stylised
representation of the interface is the highest performing hero visual, because
it answers the implicit question of what the thing actually looks like, and a
stylised crop that highlights one feature beats a raw export. A 3D metaphor of
a monitor is one abstraction layer further from that than a picture of the
actual screen.

On typography: the 2026 direction is viewport-scaled display type that reaches
the edges, and single-hue palettes pushed to extremes. This site has committed
to both ideas and executed neither fully, which is why it reads as restrained
rather than as confident.

Sources for this section are listed at the end.

## 11. What was done, 26 August 2026

Worked on branch `claude/visual-audit-2026-08-26`. Every check in the repo was
run against the result: 30 unit tests, 39 browser tests, `check_contrast.py`,
`check-spacing`, `check-copy`, `check-scene-palette`, `check-bundle`, typecheck
and lint.

### The four owner decisions, and the answers

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Which theme is the site | Follow the browser, fall back to dark, and fix the dark scheme properly. The mechanism already followed `prefers-color-scheme`; what changed is that the DARK roles now sit on bare `:root` and light is the media override, so dark is the declared default, and `color-scheme` leads with `dark`. In practice every current browser answers the query with a concrete light or dark, so this decides the rendering only where media queries do not reach. |
| The hero's picture | The owner supplied a running instance of the REVENTO system, which is the `crm` case study, and released it for use. The other two case studies wait on written client approval and keep their diagram until it comes. |
| The crossroads | Keep four objects, fix the framing. |
| The viridian field | Left to recommendation: the ink bookends survive and the green becomes a ground in three smaller places. A full viridian slab on the closing CTA remains available and is not taken. |

### The ranked list

| # | Change | What happened |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Product screenshots | Two, captured from the running REVENTO app by `web/tools/shoot-revento.mjs`, both of public signed-out pages: the tariff run for the homepage hero, the front page for the `/projekte` case study. Both sit in a new dark browser-chrome frame (`Shot`, `.shot`). The hero commits its fold to the picture at the container's full width, so it arrives near 1:1 and every figure on it is readable. **The mobile homepage now renders three images where it rendered none.** Nothing behind the login was photographed. |
| 2 | The dark palette | Rebuilt as a four-value ladder: ink 7.7, page 11.7, tint 16.5, raised 25.2 CIE L\*, ascending, with ink the floor. ### What the measurements refused

**The junction shot cannot be made to fill the frame (6.2, item 1).** Projected
against every solid at all three canvases, the four objects occupy 18.2% of the
composed half-frame vertically. A sweep of 240 camera positions and lenses that
keep everything inside tops out at 19.1%, because the free region is a portrait
column: `fovFor()` takes the horizontal requirement every time, and the
horizontal requirement is the width of the fan. Shortening the lanes does lift
the fill to 28.9% at `dist` 10, and it destroys the close-ups the audit praises,
taking a neighbour's share of a close-up from 0.9% to 41% against a 1% cap. So
the shot buys what was available instead: the band is CENTRED rather than
enlarged, moving from 18.7% above centre to 8.6%, with the subject going from
87.4% of the composed width to 89.2%. The numbers are recorded above
`WIDE_FIT_H` in `scene.ts`.

**`border` was not raised to 3:1 (4.3).** See item 3 above. The rule 1.4.11
states is about controls, so the roles were split rather than the tier moved.

**The portrait still does not appear on the homepage (6.4).** The hero's own
comment records that a portrait and a facts strip sat under the buttons for an
afternoon and the owner removed them. That is an owner decision and it stands.

**The scrollbar stays hidden (7.6).** Owner's call, 2026-08-25, restated in
`globals.css`. What answers it here is section rhythm and the dark ladder, both
of which the audit named as the alternative.

### Costs worth stating

The homepage grew from 5,446 px to 6,453 px at 1440x900, and from 6,750 px to
8,009 px at 390x844. Almost all of it is the hero picture, the phone poster and
the taller phone diagram. That is the price of the first finding, and it is
paid in content rather than in padding.
