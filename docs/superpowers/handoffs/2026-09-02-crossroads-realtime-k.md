## The design in one paragraph

The section opens on the whole K from a raised three-quarter camera, a dark floor with the strokes as soft green light, the hub disc brightest, an object on each node with a label chip. Inside the pinned track the scroll flies the camera down a stroke to its node, back through the hub, down the next, with five snapping stops (map, then 01 Website on the stem's far end, 02 App on the upper arm, 03 Capacity on the lower arm, 04 Care on the stem's near end). The pointer tilts the view and carries a light; rows, chips and objects are live. Depth of field and bloom in post. Light is baked in Cycles into per-object lightmaps and one floor texture, so three.js shows the render's look with one runtime light. Screens are drawn per language at runtime from `textures.ts`. Phones get the poster, browsers without WebGL get the stills component that exists today. Budget: scene JavaScript under 260 kB gzipped deferred, assets under 1.5 MB at 1x lazy, 60 fps on the owner's laptop (Intel Lunar Lake integrated graphics). The spec's section 9 fixes the build order: models and Cycles renders in front of the owner first, then baking and a static viewer beside the renders, then the runtime, then gates and the pull request.

## What exists to build on

- `web/tools/blender/crossroads.py`: builds the lanes, objects and shots in Blender by `bpy` script, renders transparent stills with alpha fades and a far floor that fades to nothing (never mist to a colour: the dark theme paints `#141814`, the light theme `#1c201c`), writes label anchors per shot. `--layout k --k-rotate 0.3` lays the routes out as the mark (numbers in spec section 3); `--cam x,y,z --look x,y,z` (three.js coordinates) override the junction camera. The preview that convinced the owner was rendered with `--cam 14,32,30 --look 6,0,0 --frame full --scale 0.5`. Coordinates: three.js (x, y, z) is Blender (x, -z, y); an angle about three's y is the same angle about Blender's z.
- `web/tools/blender/capture-textures.mjs`: compiles `textures.ts`, `labels.ts`, `palette.ts` with `tsc`, draws the three mock screens on the built page in their own faces (Archivo, IBM Plex Sans, IBM Plex Mono, loaded from Google Fonts, so it needs the network) and writes `tools/blender/textures/canvas-1280x800.png`, `canvas-1280x720.png`, `canvas-512x320.png`. Needs `npm run build` first and serves `out/` on 127.0.0.1:4173 itself.
- `web/tools/blender/emit-stills.mjs`: WebPs, poster crops and the generated `src/components/crossroads/stills.ts`. The README in the repo root has the recipe.
- `web/src/components/crossroads/index.tsx` (the stills component with the contain transform, the label placement rules, the pinned track from `track.ts`, the `hinted` chip state), `web/src/app/globals.css` (the crossroads block), `web/tests/e2e/crossroads.spec.ts` (38 browser tests including the track walk and the snap landing), `web/tools/shoot.mjs` (shots at 1024x736, 1440x900, 1920x1080; `--dark` for the dark theme).
- `web/scripts/check-bundle.mjs` (eager baseline 149.2 kB, refuses `BufferGeometry` in any chunk, caps the pictures at 768 kB). It will need rewriting for the real-time scene: a deferred cap for the scene chunk, an assets cap, and the eager baseline kept.
- Blender 4.5.13 LTS, portable, at `~/opt/blender/blender`, CPU Cycles only. About 30 seconds for an 808x998 still at 96 samples, two minutes at 2x, 128 samples. Run it headless with `-b -P script.py -- <args>` and in the background with nohup for anything over a minute; poll a log line such as `Blender quit`, never `pgrep -f` on the script name (the waiter's own command line matches).
- Node 22, sharp resolves through Next's tree from `web/`. Playwright's unit config runs without a browser; the e2e config serves `out/` on port 4173 (build first, about a minute). On this desktop the parallel browser run sometimes flakes under load; `--workers=1` is acceptable.
