# KluCode website audit · 25 August 2026

Audited: https://iebo13.github.io/klucode/de/ (the GitHub Pages preview of
klucode.de, branch `claude/audit-2026-08-25`, commit ee89690).

Method: every page opened in a real Chromium via Playwright at 1440×900,
1366×657 (a 1366×768 laptop with browser chrome), 1024×768 (iPad landscape)
and 390×844 (phone). The 3D section was scrubbed to seven scroll positions and
screenshotted at each. Frame times were sampled while scrolling through it.
Lighthouse 12 was run against the live URL in mobile emulation. The source in
`web/src` was read alongside, so this audit says what the site *does*, not
what it looks like it does. Market comparison in section 7 comes from a web
research pass done the same day. Screenshots referenced below are in the
session scratchpad and are not committed.

The stance is deliberately critical, as asked. Where something is good it is
said once and briefly. Where the verdict is a judgement rather than a
measurement, it is marked as such.

> Status, same day: the P0, P1 and P2 items in section 8 were worked into the
> codebase on 2026-08-25. What could not be done in code (a testimonial,
> client-approved metrics and screenshots, a WhatsApp number or booking URL,
> the employer behind „4 Jahre Frontend-Entwicklung") is left as slots in the
> content and profile and noted in the README.

## 1. Verdict in one screen

The site is technically excellent and commercially under-powered. It loads
fast, passes accessibility, has no third-party requests, has clean copy in a
consistent voice, and its code is unusually well reasoned. None of that is the
problem. The problem is that the homepage spends its single biggest bet, a
2,700 px pinned 3D section, on a decoration that most of its customers never
see, that says less than the plain list it replaces, and that costs the
visitor 1,800 px of scrolling in which nothing new is learned. Meanwhile the
things this market actually converts on are still missing or placeholdered:
screenshots, a face on the homepage, a single testimonial, and a way to
contact you that is not email.

Top ten findings, ranked by impact on a first-time visitor who arrived from a
Google search for „Webentwicklung Düsseldorf":

| # | Finding | Severity | Where |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | The 3D crossroads is a 300 svh scroll-jack that shows *less* information than its own fallback, is invisible on phones, tablets in portrait and 1366×768 laptops, and spends most of its scroll in transit frames where the open row and the framed object disagree. | Critical | Home §3 |
| 2 | No proof imagery anywhere. Two „Screenshot folgt" placeholders on /projekte, a text-only flagship panel on the homepage, a hero made entirely of type. | Critical | Home, Projekte |
| 3 | A draft offer („VORSCHLAG · NOCH NICHT BUCHBAR", 4.500 €) is live on the public services page. Internal state is leaking to customers. | High | Leistungen |
| 4 | Zero social proof. No testimonial, no client name, no number a client approved. /projekte closes with a box that apologises for this. | High | Projekte, Home |
| 5 | The homepage hides the founder. The portrait exists on /ueber-mich and is the single strongest trust device in this segment. | High | Home |
| 6 | Email is the only contact channel. The form is a mailto handoff on the preview and a PHP post on production, with no phone and no WhatsApp. For Handwerk buyers that is a wall. | High | Kontakt, site-wide |
| 7 | The same closing CTA block (identical H2, lead, two buttons, email) is repeated on six pages, and on /projekte it links to itself. The user has already flagged repeated sections. | Medium | Site-wide |
| 8 | „Die Ausgangslage" sits between the proof and the prices and argues against agencies and website kits. It interrupts the buying sequence and reads as competitor-bashing. | Medium | Home |
| 9 | /leistungen has two orphan sections: a heading-less one-sentence triage section and a section whose entire content is a link to the homepage FAQ. | Medium | Leistungen |
| 10 | A dark/light theme toggle and a language switch in the primary nav are developer chrome, not customer controls. They cost attention in the one place the site can least afford it. | Low | Header |

What is genuinely strong and should be protected: the fixed-price positioning
with a real included/not-included list, the honest AI answer on /ansatz, the
„Was ich nicht mache" list, the FAQ questions (they are the right six), the
type system after the Schibsted change, and the performance floor.

## 3. The 3D crossroads, critically

### 3.1 What it is

`#services` on the homepage. A 300 svh track with a sticky 100 svh stage. A
full-bleed three.js canvas, a glass copy panel on the left listing the four
services with prices, four DOM labels anchored to four low-poly objects (a
monitor with a landing page, a monitor with a dashboard plus a database
cylinder and a rack, an office with three desks, a server rack under a cloud).
Six camera stops driven by scroll progress: junction, way 01, 02, 03, 04,
release. The row whose way the camera is on opens a three-line detail. Under
1024×736, or with reduced motion, or without WebGL, the section renders as a
plain list with a poster image.

### 3.2 What it does well

Honestly: a lot, at the engineering level. three.js is deferred and never
enters First Load JS. Every word is in the DOM once. The labels are HTML, not
textures. The camera is composed against the free region so the panel never
covers the subject at a stop. The fallback is complete rather than degraded.
It runs at 60 fps. There is a framing test suite that projects every object at
every stop. This is the most carefully built scroll scene I have audited. That
is exactly why the UX verdict below is uncomfortable: the problem is not
execution, it is the brief.

### 3.3 Why it is bad UX, with evidence

**It shows less than its fallback.** In the enhanced state only the focused
row opens its detail (`forWhom`, `priceNote`, `reads`). At progress 0 and at
progress 1, which are the two states a normal scroll lands on and leaves
from, no row is focused and no detail is visible. The phone fallback shows
all four details at once. A visitor with a 27-inch monitor and a GPU gets a
strictly smaller information set than a visitor on a €150 Android phone. A
section's enhanced mode has to add information, not subtract it.

**Most of the scroll is transit, and in transit the section contradicts
itself.** The camera is scrubbed by scroll, so the four „stops" are four
instants in 1,800 px of travel, and the rest is movement. Screenshots at
p = 0.30 and p = 0.50 show it: at 0.30 the open row is 01 (Website), the
frame is dominated by the 02 dashboard, and the visible label says
„02 Individuelle Web-Anwendung". At 0.50 the open row is 02, the frame is the
03 office, and the label says 03. The handover constant (`FOCUS_HANDOVER =
0.78`) was tuned so the *label* follows the camera, but the *row* still uses
the same rule, so the row and the picture disagree for roughly two-thirds of
every move. A reader scrolling at normal wheel speed sees this state far more
than the stops.

**Its audience cannot see it.** The mount floor is 1024×736. The most common
Windows laptop viewport (1366×768 minus browser chrome, measured here at
1366×657) does not qualify. Every phone does not qualify. Every portrait
tablet does not qualify. For a local B2B site the mobile share is typically
55 to 70 %, and a good share of the desktop rest is exactly those laptops.
The spec itself says this is „the decision most worth revisiting". The
practical result is two products: a showpiece for people with large monitors
(designers, other developers, you) and a plain list for the people who pay.

**The fallback poster is unreadable on the device that gets it.** The
1600×378 strip renders at 327×78 px on a phone. It reads as a dark banner.
Either it needs a different crop per breakpoint or it should not be there.

**It costs 1,800 px of scroll to say what fits in one screen.** The entire
content of the section is four names, four prices, and twelve short lines of
detail. That is one viewport of a normal list. NN/g's finding on
scroll-jacking is that task-focused readers experience it as the page taking
control of their input. Here the reader is at the moment of highest intent,
the prices, and the page asks for eighteen wheel notches to let them read
three lines per offer. The spec's own measurement history (560 → 420 → 300
svh) shows the number was pushed down until it stopped hurting, not until it
earned its place.

**The objects are toys.** This is a judgement, but a confident one. The
mock landing page and mock dashboard on the two monitors are good and
specific. The office (three orange desks, three blue-headed mannequins), the
database cylinder, the grey rack and the cloud with a dotted line under it are
low-poly clip art. They sit halfway between abstract and real, which is the
one register that reads as neither premium nor deliberate. The brand
positioning is „klug gebaut", considered, senior. The objects say „asset
pack". Award-tier 3D sites go photoreal, or fully abstract, or stylised with
real art direction. Half-fidelity is the worst of the three.

**The metaphor makes the reader decode instead of read.** „Vier Wege" is
already a figure of speech, and the section literalises it into roads. To get
the information a Handwerk owner needs (what do you do, what does it cost),
they must map a monitor to „Website", a dashboard to „Web-Anwendung", an
empty chair to „Entwickler-Kapazität" and a rack under a cloud to „Betrieb &
Wartung". The list next to it already says all of that in words. The scene
adds a decoding step to information the page has already given in plain
text, which is the definition of decoration that encodes nothing.

**It is not interactive, and nothing about it is clickable.** Four objects,
four services, four detail pages, and no object, no label and no row is a
link. The labels are `aria-hidden` and purely presentational. A reader who
sees the dashboard and thinks „that one" has nothing to click. The single
link is „Alle Leistungen und Preise" at the bottom of the panel, which the
fixed header cuts in half at the release position (see the release
screenshot: „Alle Leistungen und Preise" is sliced under the capsule).

**The copy panel occludes the world.** At p = 0.12 and 0.30 the landing-page
monitor slides half under the copy panel. The gutter veil is a visible
lighter-to-darker band on the left. Both are consequences of putting a
416 px opaque panel over a scene that was composed to be full bleed.

**The release is dead space.** After the stage unsticks there is ~130 px of
empty ink before the next section, and the closing shot pulls the camera
back to the establishing view, which is the shot the reader has already seen
at the start. The section ends where it began.

**Performance is not the objection, but it is not free either.** 148 kB
gzipped of deferred JavaScript and a warm GPU for the length of the visit, on
a site whose services page promises „Ladezeit unter einer Sekunde". The
budget is respected. The principle is dented.

### 3.4 Keep, change or kill

Recommendation: **do not ship it as a pinned, scroll-driven section.** Not
because 3D is wrong for this site, but because scroll-coupling it to the
price list is the one arrangement in which it can only subtract. Three
viable directions, in order of preference. They are not exclusive.

**A. Unpin it and make it hover-driven (recommended, smallest change).**
Turn `#services` into a normal-height section: the four rows on the left with
every detail always open and every row a link to `/leistungen#key`. The
canvas stays, full bleed behind or beside, but the camera no longer reads
scroll. It idles at the establishing shot and glides to way N when row N is
hovered or focused (keyboard focus counts, so this becomes accessible for
free), returning to the wide shot on blur. Cost to the reader: zero scroll.
Cost to the code: mostly deletion. `progressOf` and the track go away, the
handle gets `focus(n)` instead of `set(p)`, and the easing already exists in
`segmentAt`. Mobile gets the list and no canvas. Labels become the row's own
hover state, not a second layer. This keeps the craft and removes the cost.

**B. Move the 3D to the hero.** The hero is the only place where a
full-bleed, gently idling scene is a convention rather than an interruption
(Linear, Vercel, most studio sites). A slow orbit around the junction, no
scroll coupling, type over it as now. `#services` becomes a plain section.
This gives the site the visual it currently lacks above the fold. Risk: the
objects' fidelity problem becomes the first thing every visitor sees, so
this direction needs either better models or a more abstract scene.

**C. Replace the metaphor with the real thing.** The site's actual missing
visual is screenshots of the CRM, the POS and the landing page. The moment
the client approvals arrive, the strongest version of this section is four
rows next to a real interface, not a rendered stand-in for one. The mock
dashboard texture in the scene is already a good facsimile of the flagship,
which is a sign of what the section wants to be.

**D. Pre-render it.** Render the four camera moves once with
`tools/shoot.mjs` into a WebP frame sequence or a short webm, and ship that
instead of three.js. This is Apple's method and it removes 148 kB of
JavaScript, the GPU cost and the mount floor in one move: a phone can play a
400 kB webm. It pairs with A (poster idles, hover plays the move to way N)
or B (a slow loop in the hero). It does not fix the toy-fidelity problem,
but it makes fixing it a one-time render rather than a runtime budget.

**E. Make „vier Wege" interactive in the way the segment expects.** A
three-question „Welcher Weg passt?" wizard (Haben Sie schon eine Website?
Hängt ein Ablauf heute in Excel oder WhatsApp? Brauchen Sie jemanden im
Team oder ein fertiges Ergebnis?) that lands on one of the four rows and
pre-fills the contact form. Competitors in this segment run configurators
for exactly this reason. It turns the metaphor into a decision aid and
gives the contact form a warm start. Never the only presentation of the
four, always beside the list.

**If it must stay scroll-driven**, the minimum set of fixes: cut the track to
150 svh at most, drop the release stop, make the row and the label hand over
at the same instant, keep all four details open at all times, make every row
and every label a link, add a visible progress indicator with four clickable
dots, use `scroll-snap` on the stops so transit frames are rare, replace the
phone poster with a per-breakpoint crop or nothing, and lower the mount
floor to a width-only query with the panel allowed to scroll internally.
That is a lot of engineering to make an idea less bad, which is the tell.

## 4. The user journey, page by page

Two personas were walked through the site. **A**, a Handwerk or small-trade
owner in the Rheinland, on a phone, arrived from Google, wants to know what
this is, what it costs and whether the person is real. **B**, an agency or
product lead on a laptop, arrived from LinkedIn, wants to know whether this
developer can drop into a team, at what day rate, with what stack.

### 4.1 Home

Current order: Hero → Projekte (proof) → Die Ausgangslage (problem) →
Leistungen (3D crossroads) → Ansatz → FAQ → Final CTA → Footer.

What works: proof before price is the right call and matches what the
stronger competitors do. The FAQ questions are the right six and each links
onward. The single primary button is correct.

What does not:

- **The hero is one viewport of type.** 96 px H1 on desktop, five lines at
  46 px on a phone, then a lead, then a button, then chips. There is no face,
  no product, no place. Persona A has no signal that a human is behind this
  until page four of the nav. Persona B has no signal of what the work looks
  like. The portrait on /ueber-mich should be here, small, with the name and
  „Düsseldorf" next to it. This is the cheapest high-value change on the
  site.
- **The proof section has no proof.** Three text cards and a topology
  diagram. „Drei Systeme, die täglich laufen" followed by nothing you can
  see running. Until screenshots exist, the flagship panel should at least
  carry the client-approved outcome number the code already has a slot for
  (`flagship.metric`), or a before/after pair in the client's own words.
- **Die Ausgangslage is in the wrong place and the wrong register.** Between
  „here is what I built" and „here is what it costs" the page stops to argue
  that agencies are slow, kits are shallow and Excel breaks. Persona A did
  not ask. The striking-through of the three options as you scroll is a
  gimmick that reads as broken text to anyone who does not notice it
  animating. Either move it below the prices as „Warum nicht einfach eine
  Agentur?" in the FAQ, or fold the one good sentence („Der Preis steht
  fest, bevor die erste Zeile geschrieben ist") into the services section.
- **Leistungen is 3,000 px down and 2,700 px long.** See section 3. For
  persona A the prices are the point of the visit. They should be reachable
  in one or two swipes, not after 3.5 phone viewports.
- **Ansatz is a duplicate.** The four steps here are the same four steps as
  /ansatz, and the „Woran ich mich halte" cards there overlap with three of
  the six FAQ answers here. One home section summarising the process in one
  line per step, linking out, is enough.
- **The final CTA is the same block as on every other page**, see 4.7.

Recommended order: Hero (with portrait) → Leistungen (unpinned, four rows,
prices, links) → Projekte (with images or a metric) → Ansatz (one line per
step) → FAQ (absorbing the Ausgangslage argument as one or two questions) →
Final CTA. Six sections instead of seven, and the prices move from 3,000 px
to under 1,000 px.

### 4.2 Leistungen

Current order: Hero → five cards (four offers plus the draft) → Abrechnung
+ Was ich nicht mache → triage sentence → „Noch Fragen zum Preis?" → Final
CTA.

- **Remove the draft card before this goes live.** „VORSCHLAG · NOCH NICHT
  BUCHBAR" with a price of 4.500 € is an internal decision rendered for
  customers. It also contradicts the page's own H1 („Vier Leistungen") and
  the homepage („Vier Wege"), which now count five cards. Ship the middle
  rung or hide it behind a flag. Nothing in between.
- **The triage sentence is an orphan section.** 274 px, no heading, one
  sentence („Unsicher, welcher der vier Wege passt?"). It is good copy in the
  wrong container. Fold it into the Final CTA lead on this page.
- **„Noch Fragen zum Preis?" is a section whose only content is a link to
  another page.** A price-shopper who has read five cards and the billing
  terms is asked to leave the page to read the answers. Render the three
  price FAQs inline here (they are already in the content file) and keep the
  link for the rest.
- **The included list is the right device, the excluded list is only
  site-wide.** „Was ich nicht mache" is excellent and should stay. But each
  card would be stronger with two or three „nicht enthalten" lines of its
  own (hosting? content writing? photography? logo?). That is what „Festpreis
  heißt Festpreis" means to a Handwerk buyer, and it is the single most
  common trust device in this market.
- Order after the fix: Hero → four cards (each with included and not
  included, an example link, and the price) → Festpreis, nicht Stunden +
  Was ich nicht mache → three price FAQs inline → Final CTA with the triage
  sentence as its lead.

### 4.3 Projekte

Current order: Hero → CRM → POS → Landingpage → „Zu Zahlen und Namen" box →
Final CTA.

- **Two visible placeholders.** The CRM and the landing page have a dotted box
  reading „Screenshot folgt, sobald der Auftraggeber ihn freigegeben hat."
  The POS has no image slot at all. Three projects, three different visual
  treatments, two of which are apologies. Until the approvals land: a
  redacted or blurred crop, a wireframe, the system diagram, or no figure
  slot at all. An empty box with an excuse is worse than a text-only card.
- **„Zu Zahlen und Namen" is a second apology.** A panel explaining that
  numbers and testimonials will come later. Delete it. The lead of the page
  already says client names are only used with written consent, which is the
  right one-sentence version.
- **The Final CTA on this page offers „Projekte ansehen"** as its secondary
  action. That is a link to the page the reader is on.
- Order is otherwise right: largest project first, before/after/result is
  the correct structure, and each card links to its matching offer. Keep
  that. Add the matching offer's price to the link („Passendes Angebot:
  Individuelle Web-Anwendung, ab 9.000 €") so the page does double duty.

### 4.4 Ansatz

Current order: Hero → four steps → „Wie ich so schnell arbeite" (the AI
disclosure) → „Woran ich mich halte" → Final CTA.

- This is the best-ordered page on the site. The AI answer is honest and
  placed correctly: after the process, before the principles.
- The whole page is on ink (dark) in the screenshots taken with the dark
  theme active. In the light theme the body is paper. Fine either way.
- „Woran ich mich halte" duplicates three FAQ answers on the homepage (code
  ownership, no exotic tech, availability). Keep the four cards here, since
  this is the page about how you work, and trim the homepage FAQ to the
  questions about money and time.
- Missing: one line at the top of the steps saying how long the whole thing
  takes end to end. The numbers exist (2 to 3 weeks, 4 to 10 weeks) but only
  on the offer cards.

### 4.5 Über mich

Current order: Hero → bio, portrait, fact list → Final CTA.

- Short, honest, right. „Eine Person, kein Wir" is a good H1.
- The bio mentions the three projects and does not link to them. Link them.
- The fact list says „4 Jahre Frontend-Entwicklung" without saying where.
  Persona B will want the employer or at least the domain. If it cannot be
  named, say what kind of company it was.
- The portrait is the only photograph on the site and it is on the least
  visited page. See 4.1.

### 4.6 Kontakt

Current order: Hero → intro + Direkt (email) + Was danach passiert | form.

- The two-column layout is right and „Was danach passiert" is the best
  section on the page. Keep it.
- **Email is the only channel.** No phone (owner's decision, recorded in
  `profile.ts`), no WhatsApp, no booking link. For persona A, WhatsApp is the
  default business channel and a 30-minute call is the offer, so the absence
  of any way to *make* that call is a gap. If a phone number must stay off
  the site, a Calendly-style slot picker or a WhatsApp Business link solves
  it without publishing a number.
- On the preview the form is a `mailto:` handoff. On production it posts to
  `/contact.php`. The note under the form on the preview explains the
  handoff, which is honest, but a reader on a work laptop with no mail client
  still hits a dead end. Production behaviour is fine. Make sure the preview
  never gets shared with a client.
- The hero lead („30 Minuten, unverbindlich, ohne Präsentation. Ich melde
  mich werktags innerhalb von 24 Stunden.") and the form column intro say the
  same thing twice within 200 px.
- The consent checkbox text is correct for DSGVO. Keep it.

### 4.7 Site-wide

- **The Final CTA block is the same on six pages.** Same H2 („Erzählen Sie
  mir, was gerade nicht läuft"), same lead, same two actions, same email. It
  is also the H1 of /kontakt. Repetition was already on the user's dislike
  list. Vary the lead per page (on /leistungen: the triage sentence, on
  /projekte: „Ähnliches Problem?", on /ansatz: „Passt das zu Ihnen?") and
  drop the secondary „Projekte ansehen" everywhere except the homepage.
- **The header carries six controls plus the logo.** Four nav items, EN,
  theme toggle, Kontakt. The theme toggle is a developer's control. Persona
  A has never toggled a theme on a business website and never will. The
  language switch is defensible (persona B may be English-speaking) but it
  belongs in the footer for a Düsseldorf Handwerk audience. Fewer controls
  in the capsule means the Kontakt button gets the attention.
- **The fixed glass capsule overlaps content.** 69 px of the viewport is
  taken on every page, and at the crossroads release the panel's link is
  cut under it. Either the header should hide on scroll-down and return on
  scroll-up, or content needs scroll-margin.
- **No location signal beyond the eyebrow.** „Düsseldorf" appears in the
  hero eyebrow and the footer. For a local search intent the city should be
  in the H1 or lead, on the About page as a sentence, and in the contact
  section as an address line (which the Impressum already requires).
- The footer note „Diese Seite: statisch ausgeliefert, keine Cookies, keine
  fremden Server" is a good line for persona B and meaningless for persona
  A. Keep it, it is small.

## 5. Findings by category

### 5.1 Content and trust

- No testimonial anywhere. One sentence from one client with a name and a
  town would outperform the entire 3D section. Every stronger competitor in
  the segment has at least three.
- No client logo, no client name, no approved number. The code has the
  slots (`flagship.metric`, `shotPending`). Fill one of them before launch or
  remove the visual promise.
- The AI disclosure is well handled and placed. Do not move it to the
  homepage.
- „Freie Kapazität ab September" is a good scarcity line, but on 25 August
  it reads as „next week". It does not roll forward: `availabilityIsStale`
  in `profile.ts` is a pre-flight check that fails the build once the month
  is past, so a stale line can never ship, but every month it has to be
  bumped by hand. Acceptable, as long as someone owns it.
- The German is consistent, formal (Sie), and free of the AI tells the user
  dislikes. Two exceptions: „Also weiter wie bisher" as a card title is
  awkward, and the phrase „hört genau da auf, wo Ihr Geschäft anfängt"
  appears in a card body and reads like ad copy.

### 5.2 Information architecture and navigation

- Five nav items plus Kontakt is right. „Ansatz" is a weak label for persona
  A (they would look for „So läuft es ab" or „Ablauf"). Consider „Ablauf".
- The homepage FAQ links each answer to a deeper page. Good. The reverse is
  missing: /leistungen and /projekte do not link back to the FAQ except via
  the orphan section.
- The prices are on the homepage and on /leistungen. Good. They are not in
  the meta description of the homepage. The services description has them.
  Consider putting the entry price in the homepage description too, since
  that is the query intent.

### 5.3 Visual design

- The Schibsted display face and the viridian on ink are distinctive and
  consistent. The type scale on the homepage hero is too large for the
  amount of visual support around it: 96 px of text with nothing to balance
  it reads as a poster, not a page.
- The ink-frame concept (ink hero, paper middle, ink close) works. It also
  means the crossroads, a second ink slab in the middle, breaks the frame.
  Removing or unpinning it restores the concept.
- Glass chips in the hero are decorative. Three text chips with a dot each
  are the weakest form of proof: a claim without a source. The same three
  facts as a single line under the portrait would carry more.
- The 3D objects' fidelity is discussed in 3.3. If the scene stays in any
  form, the office and the cloud need to be either abstracted (wireframe
  throughout, which the blueprint mechanic already supports) or modelled
  properly. Not both in one scene.
- The dark theme turns every paper section dark. The site then has no
  contrast between sections at all. If the toggle stays, the paper sections
  should stay light in both themes and only the chrome should flip.

### 5.4 Accessibility

- Lighthouse 100, a real skip link, one H1 per page, a sensible heading
  tree, all four service details in the accessibility tree even when
  collapsed, labels `aria-hidden` and deduplicated. This is above the bar.
- The enhanced services section discloses information by scroll position
  only. A keyboard user cannot open the detail of a row because rows are
  not focusable. Direction A in 3.4 fixes this (hover and focus become the
  same input).
- Muted text on the glass panel (`text-ink-muted` over blurred ink) should
  be contrast-checked against the brightest frame of the scene. The spec
  says this was bounded, not measured against WCAG. Measure it.
- `prefers-reduced-motion` is honoured by not mounting the scene. Correct.
  The Ausgangslage strike-through and the header animation should be
  checked under the same query.

### 5.5 Performance and SEO

- The numbers are good and the discipline (bundle gate, no third parties,
  self-hosted fonts) is rare. Protect it.
- 117 kB of fonts is the biggest first-paint cost. Check the subsets: 48 kB
  for one weight of Inter suggests a full Latin Extended set. A Latin-only
  subset with the German characters would halve it.
- Next prefetches six route payloads on load (~50 kB). Harmless on desktop,
  worth `prefetch={false}` on the footer links for phones.
- `noindex` on the preview is correct and the reason for SEO 66. Make sure
  the production build is verified to drop it (it is conditional on
  `NEXT_PUBLIC_SITE_URL`, which means an unset variable is production, so a
  misconfigured CI could index the preview). A one-line check in
  `check-meta.mjs` against the built HTML would close that.
- JSON-LD is present. Verify it carries `LocalBusiness` with the Düsseldorf
  address once the Impressum data is in, since that is what local search
  keys on.

### 5.6 Technical

- The crossroads has more comment than code and the comments are a record
  of decisions rather than noise. That is good practice, but the section
  has now been re-specified three times in two days (two-column → full
  bleed, pinned approach → unpinned, 560 → 420 → 300 svh). That churn is
  itself evidence that the idea is being fitted to the page by trial rather
  than by need.
- `check-bundle.mjs`, `check-copy.mjs` and the framing spec are excellent
  guards. If direction A is taken, the framing spec shrinks to five
  projections and stays.
- The contact form's preview/production split is correct but fragile: it
  keys on a single environment variable. Document it in `web/README.md`
  (the deploy section) if it is not already.

## 6. What the best sites in each tier actually do

This section rests on the research pass in section 7 and on direct
inspection of the sites named. The short version:

**Award-tier studios (Lusion, Active Theory, Basement, Unseen, Resn).** 3D is
either the hero, full bleed, idling or reacting to the pointer, or it is a
contained module inside a case study. Scroll-driven 3D is used for product
tours where the object *is* the product (Apple's iPhone pages, a shoe, a
car), because there the object changes with each step. Nobody in this tier
pins a price list. On mobile they degrade to video or a still, never to
nothing, and never to a 78 px strip.

**Developer-tool companies (Linear, Vercel, Stripe).** Full-bleed hero
visuals, always product screenshots or abstract product-derived graphics,
never a metaphor for the product. Services and pricing sections are tables
or cards with everything visible. Motion is confined to the hero and to
hover states.

**German SMB web agencies and freelancers (neuzeitwerber.de, tobeworks.de,
florian-strasser.de and peers).** No 3D anywhere. Above the fold: a face or
a real site screenshot, one sentence of positioning, a price or a „ab"
price, a phone number or WhatsApp button. Then: testimonials with names and
towns, a portfolio with screenshots, an included/not-included list per
package, an FAQ, a contact form with a phone alternative. The structure
KluCode's homepage has is close to this once the crossroads is removed and
the proof slots are filled.

The gap to close is not craft. It is that KluCode's homepage currently
resembles the first tier in ambition while serving the third tier's
customers, and it borrows the one device (pinned scroll 3D) that even the
first tier reserves for product objects.

## 7. Research notes

What the evidence says, with sources. Read this before arguing for keeping
the pin.

### 7.1 Scroll-jacking and pinned 3D

- **NN/g, Scrolljacking 101 (2023).** Usability testing across industries:
  „the majority of our study participants were at least mildly disoriented
  by scrolljacking". Some read it as a bug. Task-oriented users had the
  lowest tolerance. The worst cases „combined altered scroll rates with
  required text reading", which is precisely a pinned price list. Their
  conditions for it working: below the fold, short, no direction changes,
  minimal text inside the pin, sticky nav as an escape hatch, excluded from
  mobile. https://www.nngroup.com/articles/scrolljacking-101/
- **Controlled experiment, HCI International (Springer, 2025).** Two
  identical prototypes, one scroll-jacked, 20 participants, task-driven.
  No speed difference, but significantly lower accuracy and lower
  satisfaction with the scroll-jacked version. Small sample, same direction
  as NN/g. https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-032-16454-4_6
- **NN/g, What Parallax Lacks (2019).** Goal-oriented users scroll past
  scroll-driven content before it finishes. Designers appreciate the craft,
  users are indifferent. Keep effects on backgrounds, offer in-page links.
  https://www.nngroup.com/articles/parallax-usability/
- **NN/g, Scrolling and Attention (2018).** 57 % of viewing time is above
  the fold, 74 % within the first two screenfuls, users rarely pass the
  third. On this homepage the crossroads starts at the third screenful and
  runs for three more, so Ansatz, FAQ and the CTA live in the attention
  tail. https://www.nngroup.com/articles/scrolling-and-attention/
- **Baymard.** Inline scroll hijacking produced „a lack of page control" and
  traps touch users. 70 % of mobile users scroll the whole homepage to get
  an overview, so the offer breadth must be exposed fast.
  https://baymard.com/blog/inline-scroll-areas and
  https://baymard.com/blog/mobile-homepage-usability
- **Accessibility.** WCAG 2.3.3 names parallax and scroll-triggered
  animation explicitly, `prefers-reduced-motion` is the sufficient
  technique. Up to 35 % of adults over 40 have some vestibular dysfunction,
  and only a minority of them have the OS flag set, so the reduced-motion
  fallback protects fewer people than it looks like.
  https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG21/Understanding/animation-from-interactions.html,
  https://www.a11yproject.com/posts/understanding-vestibular-disorders/,
  https://www.joshwcomeau.com/animation/scroll-driven-animations/
- **When it works.** One to two screens, fast, below the fold, progressive
  disclosure of a *visual* that changes at each step, no reading required
  inside the pin. Apple's product pages are the canonical success and they
  are not live 3D: pre-rendered frames scrubbed on a canvas, one fallback
  image on slow connections.
  https://css-tricks.com/lets-make-one-of-those-fancy-scrolling-animations-used-on-apple-product-pages/

### 7.2 How best-in-class sites use 3D in 2025 to 2026

- **Linear**: no 3D, no hero video. Annotated screenshots per feature,
  named customer testimonials, „40,000+ product teams". https://linear.app
- **Stripe**: a WebGL mesh gradient as hero backdrop with a static fallback
  image, then logo wall, product cards, numbers. Nothing pinned.
  https://stripe.com
- **Vercel**: shader gradients as backdrop. The heavy WebGL went on a
  separate conference campaign page with an FPS-driven quality monitor and
  effects disabled on mobile.
  https://vercel.com/blog/building-an-interactive-webgl-experience-in-next-js
- **Apple**: scroll-scrubbed image sequences, roughly 65 frames over about
  1,200 px, static fallback on slow networks. https://jurn.io/apple-scroll-animation/
- **Lusion, basement.studio, Locomotive, Unseen**: DOM-first homepages, a
  project grid, 3D inside contained modules or in a separate labs area.
  Unseen's „project ring" is a contained 3D object, not a page-long track.
  https://lusion.co, https://basement.studio, https://locomotive.ca,
  https://tympanus.net/codrops/2026/07/20/the-craft-behind-memorable-digital-experiences-inside-unseen-studio/
- **Igloo Inc** (whole-site WebGL) is the exception that proves it: built
  for a client whose product *is* the spectacle, with continuous low-end
  device testing. https://www.awwwards.com/igloo-inc-case-study.html

Pattern: for SaaS leaders 3D is a hero backdrop with a static fallback, or
absent. For studios it is a contained module, or the whole site when
spectacle is the product. Scroll spent on 3D is one screen (backdrop) or one
to three screens (Apple sequences). Nobody in this set pins a 3D chooser
for three screens on the section where the prices live.

### 7.3 What German SMB-facing agencies and freelancers do

Checked: neuzeitwerber.de, tobeworks.de, florian-strasser.de, oleroloff.de,
davidkeiser.de, kopfundstift.de, seiten-werk.com, meinbetriebonline.de,
cconnect-webdesign.de. **3D appears on none of them.** The German agencies
with 3D capability (The Weather, chris and friends) sell to brands, not to
Handwerk.

Recurring structure, in order:

1. Hero: what plus where („Webdesign und Entwicklung in Düsseldorf"), one
   CTA, almost always „Kostenloses Erstgespräch".
2. Proof within the first two screens: Google review stars with counts
   (neuzeitwerber 5.0 from 125 reviews, seiten-werk 4.88 from 225, Roloff
   5.0 from 14), logos, numbers („100+ Projekte").
3. A pain-point section (tobeworks „Klingt das bekannt?", Keiser's
   „agency vs me" table). This is where KluCode's „Ausgangslage" belongs
   structurally, and note that they place it *after* proof and *before*
   services, which is where it is now. The objection in 4.1 is therefore
   about register and length, not position. Keep it if it is cut to one
   row per option and loses the strike-through.
4. Service cards, then process steps, then portfolio, then testimonials,
   then FAQ, then contact.
5. Prices in the open: Roloff 2,490 to 4,995 € packages, meinbetriebonline
   800 to 1,200 € fixed, kopfundstift three tiers, cconnect 1,000 to
   5,000 €. KluCode's price transparency is in line with the segment, not
   ahead of it.
6. Configurators are common: tobeworks five-step Konfigurator, kopfundstift
   price calculator, cconnect „Fragebogen und Preisrechner". A short
   „Welcher Weg passt?" wizard that ends in the contact form would be the
   segment-native way to make „vier Wege" interactive.

Skeptical read: these sites are conversion-literate and visually
interchangeable. The unhappiness the redesign is trying to solve is real.
The segment's answer to differentiation is voice and proof, not rendering.

### 7.4 Alternative patterns for „vier Wege", compared

| Pattern | For | Against | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Card grid or row list (the current fallback) | All four visible, comparable, crawlable, stacks on mobile, no motion risk | Generic unless copy carries it | NN/g on cards as entry points, https://www.nngroup.com/articles/cards-component/ |
| Comparison table | Supports the comparison a buyer actually makes, works up to five items | Mobile collapses to two columns. Rows must be scope, never price magnitude, because the four offers use three price units | https://www.nngroup.com/articles/comparison-tables/ |
| Tabs | Compact | Hides three of four, fails comparison tasks, worse than accordions on mobile | https://www.nngroup.com/articles/tabs-used-right/ |
| Decision wizard („Welcher Weg passt?") | Right for infrequent, novice decisions, which is persona A. Feeds the contact form | Must never be the only presentation | https://www.nngroup.com/articles/wizards/, tobeworks, kopfundstift |
| Sticky side nav plus scrolling detail | Native scroll kept, four anchors give orientation and an escape hatch | Long page, needs accordions on mobile | https://www.nngroup.com/articles/in-page-links-content-navigation/ |
| Contained 3D toy per card | Keeps the objects and the craft, no pin, poster until in view | Four contexts are expensive, use one shared canvas with scissor rects | Spline's own limit of three embeds per page, https://docs.spline.design/doc/how-to-optimize-your-scene/doczPMIye7Ko |
| Pre-rendered loop or frame sequence | Apple's method. Render the crossroads once, ship webm or WebP frames, zero three.js on the client, poster under reduced motion | No interactivity, but a chooser never needed it | https://css-tricks.com/lets-make-one-of-those-fancy-scrolling-animations-used-on-apple-product-pages/ |

Bottom line: the evidence weighs against a 300 svh pinned track on the
section that carries the prices. NN/g's single strongest warning is
scroll-jacking combined with text the user must read, and the crossroads is
exactly that. The lowest-risk way to keep the objects is contained (hover
driven as in direction A, one pre-rendered loop, or a toy per card) above a
plain comparable board, with an optional short wizard feeding the contact
form.
